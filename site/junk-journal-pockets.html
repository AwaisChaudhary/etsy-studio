Junk journal pockets

Six easy pockets for junk journals, each drawn so you can see how it goes together, plus a size helper that works out how big to cut a pocket for your page and the tags or cards going in it, with a template to print at actual size.

Quick answer: the easiest junk journal pocket is a piece of card with folded flaps. Cut it 1 in (2.5 cm) wider and 1/2 in (1.3 cm) taller than you want the pocket, score 1/2 in in from each side and the bottom, fold the flaps back, trim the corners and glue the flaps (not the middle) to the page. Make the pocket about 1/4 in wider than the tag or card that goes in it, and about 1 in shorter, so the top peeks out and is easy to pull. No card? Fold up the bottom of a page, cut the flap end off an envelope, or tape a scrap down on three sides with washi tape.

How to make a glue-on pocket with flaps

  1. Pick the size. Measure what goes in: the pocket should be about 1/4 in wider than it and about 1 in shorter. The helper above does the sums.
  2. Cut the card 1 in wider and 1/2 in taller than the pocket, so there's a 1/2 in flap on each side and along the bottom.
  3. Score and fold along the flap lines with a bone folder or the back of a butter knife and a ruler, then fold the flaps back.
  4. Trim the corners of the flaps at an angle so they don't overlap and make a lump.
  5. Glue only the flaps to the page. Glue in the middle seals the pocket shut. Press flat under a book for a few minutes.
  6. Decorate the top edge with a strip of washi tape, lace or a scalloped edge cut with scissors, so it doesn't tear when you pull cards out.

How to make a fold-up page pocket

When you make your signatures, cut one page taller than the rest: the extra is the pocket. Fold the extra up against the front of the page and glue or sew down the two sides. A 3 in fold makes a pocket that holds a 3 x 4 in journaling card with a good bit showing. It's the strongest pocket because it's part of the page, so it's good for the front of a signature. If your paper isn't tall enough, glue a strip to the bottom of the page first and fold that. Planning signatures? See the junk journal signature calculator.

How to make an envelope pocket

Cut the flap end off an envelope so it's open along the top, then glue the back of it to a page. Or keep the envelope whole and sew it in as a page with its opening at the side, which gives you a hidden pocket. Coloured security envelopes (the ones with patterns inside) and old airmail envelopes look great. A 4 3/8 x 5 3/4 in (A2) envelope fits a 3 x 4 in card either way round.

How to make corner pockets

Fold a square of paper in half diagonally to get a triangle, then glue it into a page corner with the fold facing inwards. Do the opposite corner the same way. Slide a photo or card in at an angle. About 2 in squares hold a 4 x 6 in photo well; make them smaller for small cards. Photo corners are the same idea, only tiny.

How to make a belly band

Cut a strip of paper or card about 1 1/2 in wide and twice the page width plus 1 in long. Wrap it round the page, overlap the ends at the back and glue them together (not to the page), so the band can slide. Tuck tags, cards and photos under it. Ribbon, lace or a strip of book page work too.

How to make a washi tape pocket

Lay a piece of paper on the page and tape down the two sides and the bottom with washi tape, leaving the top open. It takes a minute, adds no bulk, and it's the one to use in a bought notebook or a planner. Use a wide tape so it holds.

What to put in a junk journal pocket

  • Tags with journaling on the back. A #5 tag (2 3/8 x 4 3/4 in) is the classic size; a #8 tag needs a bigger page.
  • Journaling cards (3 x 4 in) and library cards (3 x 5 in).
  • Photos, tickets, receipts, letters, seed packets and other ephemera. See our list of 62 ephemera ideas.
  • Tuck spots for later: a blank card to write on when you have something to say.

Tips for pockets that last

  • Use thicker paper for the pocket than for the page: card, cereal box card covered in paper, or a doubled sheet.
  • Don't fill every page with pockets: they make the spine bulge. Leave a gap in the binding or use a wider spine.
  • Strong glue or stitches on the sides: a glue stick lifts with use. Double-sided tape, PVA or a machine zigzag holds.
  • Tea-stain new white card so it matches old pages: see how to tea stain paper.

Questions crafters ask

How do you make a pocket in a junk journal?

Cut card 1 in wider and 1/2 in taller than the pocket, fold back 1/2 in flaps on the sides and bottom, trim the corners and glue only the flaps to the page.

How big should a junk journal pocket be?

About 1/4 in wider than the tag or card going in, and about 1 in shorter so the top peeks out. For a 3 x 4 in journaling card that's a 3 1/4 x 3 in pocket.

What size tag fits in a junk journal pocket?

A #5 shipping tag (2 3/8 x 4 3/4 in) suits A5 and half letter pages. Use #3 tags for small journals and #8 tags for 6 x 9 in or larger pages.

What can I use to make junk journal pockets?

Card, envelopes, old book covers, paper bags, file folders, cereal box card covered with paper, lace, fabric or a scrap of paper and washi tape.

How do you make a hidden pocket in a junk journal?

Glue two pages together along three edges and leave one side open, or sew a whole envelope in as a page with its opening at the side.

What glue is best for junk journal pockets?

Double-sided tape or PVA glue on the flaps. Glue sticks are fine for light pockets but can lift when cards go in and out a lot.
