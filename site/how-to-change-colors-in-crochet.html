How to change colours in crochet

Change colour on the last yarn over of the stitch before, and the new colour starts clean. Here's how for every stitch, at the end of a row, mid-row and in rounds, plus a stripe planner that tells you when to carry the yarn and when to cut.

Quick answer: work the last stitch in the old colour until 2 loops are left on the hook (3 for half double). Drop the old colour, yarn over with the new colour and pull through to finish the stitch. At the end of a row, do this on the last stitch, then chain and turn. Leave both tails about 6 in (15 cm) long and weave them in later. For stripes of an even number of rows, you don't need to cut: carry the unused colour up the side.

How do I change colour for each stitch?

StitchWhen to bring in the new colour
Single crochetInsert, yarn over, pull up a loop (2 loops on the hook). Drop the old colour, then finish the stitch: yarn over with the new colour and pull through both loops.
Half double crochetYarn over, insert, yarn over, pull up a loop (3 loops on the hook). Drop the old colour and pull the new colour through all 3 loops.
Double crochetWork the stitch until 2 loops are left on the hook. Drop the old colour and pull the new colour through those last 2.
Treble crochetWork until the last 2 loops, then finish with the new colour, the same as double crochet.
Slip stitch join (rounds)Insert into the first stitch of the round, then pull the new colour through the stitch and the loop on the hook.

The last-yarn-over method is the standard one in most patterns (Crochet Spot, ChristaCo Design, MJ's Off The Hook Designs). Stitch names are US terms; in UK terms sc is dc and dc is tr. See US vs UK crochet terms.

How do I change colour at the end of a row?

  1. Work to the last stitch of the row in the old colour.
  2. Stop with the last 2 loops on the hook (3 for hdc).
  3. Drop the old colour to the back and finish the stitch with the new colour.
  4. Chain for your turning chain in the new colour, turn, and carry on.

Changing in the turning chain instead leaves a loop of the old colour on the edge, which shows. To stop the first stitches coming loose, give both tails a gentle tug, or work the first few stitches over the new tail.

How do I change colour in the middle of a row?

The same way: finish the stitch before the new colour with the new colour. If you'll need the old colour again a few stitches later, don't cut it. Lay it along the tops of the stitches you're working into and crochet over it, so it travels with you, hidden inside the stitches (this is tapestry crochet). Keep the carried strand just snug enough to lie flat: too tight and the fabric puckers, too loose and loops catch. For a big block of colour, use a separate small ball or bobbin for each area instead.

How do I change colour in rounds?

In joined rounds, finish the round in the old colour, then make the slip stitch join with the new colour: insert into the first stitch, pull the new colour through the stitch and the loop on the hook, and chain up. All your changes then line up at one spot, which you can keep at the back. In spiral rounds (amigurumi) there's no join, so the stripe always ends with a small step, called a jog. To soften it, change colour on the last yarn over, then work a slip stitch or a shorter stitch at the start of the new colour.

How do I change colours without cutting the yarn?

  • Carry up the side for stripes in rows. It works when each stripe is an even number of rows: 2, 4 or 6. Then the dropped colour is at the same edge when you need it again. With odd-row stripes, the colour ends up at the wrong edge.
  • Trap the strand. When you pick a colour up, work your turning chain or first stitch over the carried strand so it doesn't make a big loop at the edge. A border later covers it completely.
  • Carry along the row (tapestry crochet) for colour changes inside the row.
  • Carry behind the join in joined rounds.

Carry long gaps loosely. Over many rows a carried strand can pull the edge in or show through pale colours, so for a long gap between uses it's often neater to cut and rejoin. As a rule of thumb we cut once a colour would be carried more than about 8 rows (our estimate, about 3 in of edge); the planner above uses that.

Should I knot the yarn when changing colours?

You don't need to. A well woven-in tail holds better than a knot, and knots can work loose or poke through on the right side. Some crocheters tie the two tails together loosely to stop the first stitch slipping while they work, then untie it before weaving in. Or use a Russian join to join yarn of the same kind with no ends to weave.

How do I weave in the ends?

Our full guide to weaving in ends covers slippery and chunky yarn, amigurumi and the invisible fasten off. In short: leave about 6 in (15 cm). Thread the tail on a blunt yarn needle and weave it through the back of stitches of the same colour, so it doesn't show. Go one way for an inch or two, then turn and go back a short way through different stitches, and trim close. To save time, crochet over the tails as you go: hold them along the top of the row and work the next few stitches over them.

Questions crafters ask

How do you change colours in crochet?

Work the last stitch before the change in the old colour until 2 loops are left on the hook, drop the old colour, then yarn over with the new colour and pull through to finish the stitch.

Do you change colour before or after the turning chain?

Before. Change on the last yarn over of the last stitch of the row, then make the turning chain in the new colour.

How do you change colours in crochet without cutting the yarn?

For stripes with an even number of rows, carry the unused colour up the side of the work and pick it up when you need it. Inside a row, crochet over the unused colour so it travels inside the stitches.

Should I tie a knot when changing yarn colours?

It isn't needed. Weaving the ends in well holds better and doesn't make a lump. If you tie the tails to hold them while you work, untie them before weaving in.

How long should the tail be when changing colours?

About 6 in (15 cm) is plenty to weave in with a yarn needle.

How do you change colour in a spiral round?

Change on the last yarn over of the last stitch, as usual. Spirals always leave a small jog at the change; a slip stitch or shorter stitch at the start of the new colour softens it.
