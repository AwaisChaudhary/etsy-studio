How to knit a Christmas stocking for beginners

This stocking is knitted flat in two simple pieces: no double-pointed needles, no heel flap and no heel turn. If you can knit, purl and decrease, you can make it. Every step is drawn below, and the writer turns any size and yarn into a full row-by-row pattern with yarn amounts.

Quick answer: the easiest way to knit a Christmas stocking is flat, in two boot-shaped pieces. Cast on about 32 stitches in worsted yarn, knit a garter cuff, then the leg in stockinette; cast on extra stitches for the toe, knit the foot, decrease at both ends to round the heel and toe, and bind off. Knit a mirror piece, then sew them together. A classic 18 in stocking takes roughly 180 to 250 yards of worsted and 8 to 12 hours, by our estimates.

How to knit a Christmas stocking, step by step

What you need

  • Yarn: worsted (4) acrylic or wool blend in red or green for the body, plus white for the cuff, heel or toe if you like. Acrylic is cheap, washable and holds its shape. Chunky yarn knits up twice as fast.
  • Needles: straight needles, or a circular needle used flat, in the size on the yarn label: about US 8 (5 mm) for worsted, US 10.5 (6.5 mm) for chunky.
  • Also: a tapestry needle to sew it up, a few pins or clips, scissors and a tape measure.

New to knitting? Learn how to cast on and how to knit and purl first. Every other skill is explained in the steps.

Why knit a stocking flat?

A traditional stocking is knitted in the round with a heel flap, a turned heel and a gusset, just like a big sock. It looks lovely but needs double-pointed needles or magic loop, and the heel is where most beginners get stuck. Knitting two flat pieces avoids all of that: you only work straight rows, cast on a few stitches, and decrease at the edges. The heel and toe come out rounded because of the decreases. Once you've made this one, the Christmas stocking calculator gives cast-on and heel numbers for knitting one in the round.

How to add stripes, a name or a white heel and toe

  • Stripes: change colour at the start of a right-side row. Carry the yarn not in use up the side if the stripes are 4 rows or less; otherwise cut it and weave in. Our stripe generator makes random stripe orders.
  • White heel and toe: on the foot rows, knit the first and last 8 to 10 stitches in white and the middle in red, twisting the yarns where they meet so there's no hole. Or keep it simple: knit the whole foot in white.
  • A name: it's easiest to embroider the name onto the cuff afterwards with duplicate stitch or chain stitch (see our embroidery stitches). The stocking calculator checks if a name fits the cuff.

Sewing the pieces together

Weave in all the ends first, except one long tail for sewing. Put the pieces right sides together, line up the cuffs, and pin every inch or so. Back stitch about one stitch in from the edge, all the way round the leg, heel, foot and toe, leaving the top open. Turn it right side out and push the toe out gently. For an invisible seam, lay them right side up and use mattress stitch instead. For the hanging loop, knit a 3-stitch i-cord or crochet a 25-chain loop and sew both ends inside the cuff at the heel side. Need more help? See hand sewing stitches and how to weave in ends.

How long does it take to knit a Christmas stocking?

As a rough guide for a comfortable beginner (our estimates), a mini ornament stocking takes 1 to 2 hours, a child's stocking 4 to 6 and a classic 18 in stocking 8 to 12 hours in worsted, plus an hour to sew up. Chunky yarn is about a third quicker. Making several as gifts? Questions crafters ask

How do you knit a Christmas stocking for beginners?

Knit it flat in two boot-shaped pieces: cast on, knit a garter cuff, knit the leg in stockinette, cast on extra stitches for the toe, knit the foot, decrease at both ends to round the heel and toe, and bind off. Knit a mirror piece and sew them together.

Can you knit a Christmas stocking on straight needles?

Yes. The flat two-piece method uses straight needles only: no double-pointed needles, heel flap or heel turn.

How many stitches do I cast on for a Christmas stocking?

For a classic 18 in stocking knitted flat in two pieces, about 32 stitches per piece in worsted yarn (18 stitches per 4 in), or 24 in chunky. Knitted in the round it's about 64 in worsted.

How much yarn do I need to knit a Christmas stocking?

Roughly 180 to 250 yards of worsted yarn for a classic 18 in stocking, by our estimates: buy two 200-yard balls, one for the body and one for the cuff, heel and toe.

What is the best yarn for a knitted Christmas stocking?

Worsted or chunky acrylic, or a wool blend. It's sturdy, keeps its shape when filled, and comes in bright reds, greens and white.

How long does it take to knit a Christmas stocking?

About 8 to 12 hours for a classic 18 in stocking in worsted yarn for a beginner, plus an hour to sew it up (our estimates). Chunky yarn is quicker.
