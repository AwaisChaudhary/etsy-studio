How the estimate works

We start from typical amounts for each project in worsted (weight 4) yarn, then adjust for your yarn's thickness: thinner yarn needs more length, thicker yarn less. Crochet usually uses around a quarter more yarn than knitting for the same piece, and cables or bobbles use more again.

Buy from the same dye lot. Skeins dyed in different batches can look slightly different side by side. The dye lot number is on the label, so buy everything you need at once, plus one spare if you can return it.

Your pattern's yarn amount always wins. Use this when you're designing your own piece, swapping yarn, or the pattern doesn't say.

Making a blanket?

Blankets vary so much in size that a single number isn't much help. Our blanket size calculator lists the usual sizes from lovey to king, draws your blanket next to a bed, and works out the yarn from the exact width and length you want.

Using a different yarn from the pattern?

If your pattern already gives an amount but you're swapping brands, don't start from scratch: the yarn substitution calculator turns the pattern's balls into balls of your yarn and checks the thickness.

How do I work out yarn from a swatch?

  1. Weigh your swatch on a kitchen scale after knitting or crocheting it in the real yarn and stitch. A 4 in (10 cm) square is easy to measure.
  2. Work out the area of your piece and how many swatches fit into it. A 20 × 60 in scarf is 1,200 sq in, which is 75 squares of 4 × 4 in.
  3. Multiply the swatch weight by that number to get the grams you need.
  4. Turn grams into balls using the label: a 100 g ball needs grams ÷ 100, rounded up. Add about 10% for seams, edging and the swatch itself.

How many skeins should I buy?

Divide the yards you need by the yards on one skein and round up, then add one if you can. Running short is worse than having a spare: a new skein from a different dye lot can show as a stripe. Many shops take back unused skeins, so ask before you buy. Keep track of what's left over in a stash list, and the project tracker can hold the yarn details for each piece. For scarves by age and style, the scarf size chart gives the size and yarn together.

Questions crafters ask

How many skeins do I need for a blanket?

A throw of about 125 by 150 cm (50 by 60 in) usually takes around 1,800 to 2,200 yards of worsted yarn when knitted, which is often 9 to 11 skeins of 200 yards. Crochet takes more. For other blanket sizes, use the blanket size calculator.

How do I work out yarn for a project that isn't listed?

Pick a similar project in the list and adjust, or use a swatch: weigh a 10 cm (4 in) square, multiply by how many squares fit your piece to get the grams, then use the yards per gram on your yarn label. Add about 10 percent for seams and borders.

Does crochet use more yarn than knitting?

Usually yes, often about 25 to 30 percent more for the same size piece, because crochet stitches are taller and denser.

Why does thinner yarn need more yards?

Thin yarn makes small stitches, so you need many more of them, and much more length, to cover the same area.

How much yarn do I need for a beanie?

About 200 yards of worsted yarn for a knitted adult beanie, about 140 in bulky and about 240 in DK. Crochet takes roughly a quarter more. These are estimates, so check your pattern.

How much yarn do I need for a scarf?

About 400 yards of worsted for a knitted scarf about 6 ft long, or about 510 yards crocheted. Bulky yarn needs about 290 yards.
