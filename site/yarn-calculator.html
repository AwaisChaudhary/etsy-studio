How the estimate works

We start from typical amounts for each project in worsted (weight 4) yarn, then adjust for your yarn's thickness: thinner yarn needs more length, thicker yarn less. Crochet usually uses around a quarter more yarn than knitting for the same piece, and cables or bobbles use more again.

Buy from the same dye lot. Skeins dyed in different batches can look slightly different side by side. The dye lot number is on the label, so buy everything you need at once, plus one spare if you can return it.

Your pattern's yarn amount always wins. Use this when you're designing your own piece, swapping yarn, or the pattern doesn't say.

Questions crafters ask

How many skeins do I need for a blanket?

A throw of about 125 by 150 cm (50 by 60 in) usually takes around 1,800 to 2,200 yards of worsted yarn when knitted, which is often 9 to 11 skeins of 200 yards. Crochet takes more. For other blanket sizes, use the blanket size calculator.

How do I work out yarn for a project that isn't listed?

Pick a similar project in the list and adjust, or use a swatch: weigh a 10 cm (4 in) square, multiply by how many squares fit your piece to get the grams, then use the yards per gram on your yarn label. Add about 10 percent for seams and borders.

Does crochet use more yarn than knitting?

Usually yes, often about 25 to 30 percent more for the same size piece, because crochet stitches are taller and denser.

Why does thinner yarn need more yards?

Thin yarn makes small stitches, so you need many more of them, and much more length, to cover the same area.
