How to join granny squares

Five ways to join granny squares, from a simple whip stitch to join-as-you-go, each with our own diagram. Plus a calculator for how many seams your blanket has and how much yarn the joining takes.

Quick answer: block all your squares to the same size, then lay them out. For the easiest join, hold two squares right sides together, thread a tapestry needle with matching yarn and whip stitch through the back loops of each pair of matching stitches, corner to corner. Prefer to use your hook? A slip stitch or single crochet join through both edges is quicker and stronger. To skip sewing altogether, join as you go: on the last round of each new square, swap the chain spaces for slip stitches into the square next to it. Join in long rows, not one square at a time, and you'll have far fewer ends to weave in.

Ways to join granny squares

Before you join: block and lay out

  1. Weave in the ends on every square first, or leave one long tail on each to sew with. See how to weave in ends.
  2. Block the squares. Pin each one flat to the same measured size, mist with water and let it dry. Squares that match make straight seams.
  3. Lay them out on the floor or a bed and take a photo, so you remember the order once you start.
  4. Pick your joining yarn. Use the same weight as the squares. A matching colour hides the seam; a contrast colour (often the border colour) frames each square.

How to whip stitch granny squares together

  1. Hold two squares right sides together, edges lined up, corner stitches matching.
  2. Cut a length of yarn about three times the length of the seam and thread a tapestry needle.
  3. Start in the corner chain space: go through both squares and leave a 6 in tail.
  4. Work along the edge: put the needle through the back loop of the stitch on the front square and the back loop of the matching stitch on the back square, always from the same side. Pull snug, not tight.
  5. Keep going stitch by stitch to the far corner. Through back loops only, the front loops stay on top as a neat line; through both loops the seam is stronger but shows a little more.
  6. Carry on to the next pair of squares in the row without cutting the yarn.

How to join granny squares with a slip stitch

  1. Hold two squares right sides together (the ridge will be on the back) or wrong sides together (the ridge shows on the front).
  2. Make a slip knot on your hook. Insert the hook through the corner space of both squares, yarn over and pull through everything on the hook.
  3. Insert through the next pair of matching stitches (back loops for a flatter seam, both loops for a firmer one), yarn over and pull through both the squares and the loop on your hook.
  4. Repeat to the corner, then add the next pair of squares and keep going. Keep it loose: a tight slip stitch seam puckers. Going up a hook size helps.

How to join granny squares with single crochet

Hold the squares wrong sides together for a raised ridge on the front, or right sides together to hide it. Work one single crochet (UK double crochet) through both edges in each pair of stitches, and 1 sc in each corner space. This is the strongest of the hook joins, but it uses the most yarn and adds a little width. It looks great in a contrast colour.

Mattress stitch for a flat, invisible seam

Lay the two squares flat, right side up, edges touching. Bring a threaded needle up through the outer loop of the first stitch on one square, then across through the outer loop of the matching stitch on the other square. Zigzag between the two edges like the rungs of a ladder, and every few stitches pull the yarn gently so the edges close. The seam lies completely flat.

How to join granny squares as you go

Finish the first square completely. On the last round of the second square, work as usual until you reach a side that should touch the first square. Then:

  1. At the corner: work the first 3 dc, then instead of ch 2, ch 1, slip stitch into the corner space of the finished square, ch 1, and work the next 3 dc into your own corner.
  2. Along the side: instead of each ch 1 between clusters, slip stitch into the matching ch-1 space of the finished square.
  3. At the next corner do the same as the first, then finish the round as normal.
  4. Squares that touch two finished squares join on two sides; at a four-way corner, slip stitch into the join that's already there.

This is the method most granny blanket makers end up loving: the blanket grows square by square and there's nothing to sew at the end. New to the square itself? Start with how to crochet a granny square.

Join in strips to save time

Instead of joining every square to its neighbour separately, join all the squares into rows with one long seam per row, then join the rows with long seams the other way. When you cross a corner, catch both corners and keep going. A blanket 8 squares across and 10 down then has just 16 long seams instead of 142 short ones, and only about 32 ends to weave in.

Common problems

  • Seams pucker: the join is too tight. Use a hook one size up for slip stitch or sc joins, or pull sewn stitches only until they close.
  • Squares don't line up: count stitches from each corner and pin matching corners before you start. Blocking fixes small size differences.
  • Corners have holes: work into both corner chain spaces, and at four-way corners go into the join already made.
  • The seam shows too much: use matching yarn, join through the back loops, or switch to mattress stitch.

Questions crafters ask

What is the easiest way to join granny squares?

Whip stitch. Hold two squares right sides together and sew through the back loops of matching stitches with a tapestry needle. It needs no crochet skill and gives a flat seam.

What is the best way to join granny squares for a blanket?

Join as you go saves the most time because there is no sewing at the end. For squares that are already made, a slip stitch or whip stitch join in long strips is quick and neat.

Should I join granny squares right sides or wrong sides together?

Right sides together hides the seam ridge on the back. Wrong sides together puts the ridge on the front, which looks good with a single crochet join in a contrast colour.

How much yarn do I need to join granny squares?

By our rough estimate for worsted yarn, a sewn seam uses about 2.5 to 3 times its length in yarn, a slip stitch join about 4 times and a single crochet join about 6 times. Join as you go uses no extra yarn.

Do you block granny squares before joining?

Yes, if you can. Blocking makes all the squares the same size so the seams line up and the blanket lies flat.

How do you join granny squares without sewing?

Use a hook join (slip stitch or single crochet) through both edges, or join as you go on the last round of each new square.

How many seams does a granny square blanket have?

If you join in strips, one long seam between each row and column: squares across minus 1, plus squares down minus 1. An 8 by 10 blanket has 16 long seams.
