Craft fair profit calculator

Booth fee, travel, card fees, materials and your time: see what a market day really earns, how many sales you need to break even, and whether a fair is worth the fee.

Quick answer: break-even sales = (booth fee + travel, food and other fair costs) ÷ what you keep per item after materials, packaging and card fees. With a $100 booth and $40 of other costs, selling $20 items that cost $6 to make, you keep about $13.13 each, so you need 11 sales just to cover the fair. A common rule of thumb is to aim for sales of at least 3 times the booth fee.

What does a craft fair booth cost?

Fees vary a lot. TheCraftMap's 2026 guide puts small community fairs at about $25 to $75, regional fairs at $75 to $200, juried shows at $200 to $500 or more, and holiday markets at $100 to $400. Electricity is often $10 to $50 extra. School and church fairs are sometimes free or ask for a raffle prize instead. A higher fee usually means more visitors, but it doesn't promise more sales: Made Urban's sellers' guide makes the same point.

The costs people forget

  • Your time. Applying, making stock, packing, driving, setting up and selling. A 6-hour fair is easily a 12-hour day.
  • Card fees. Phone card readers in the US charge around 2.6% plus 15¢ for each tap or swipe (Square's in-person rate). Check your own reader's rate.
  • Travel and food. Fuel, parking, and lunch you'd not otherwise buy. Out-of-town shows add a hotel.
  • Display and packaging. A tent, risers, signs, bags, tissue and business cards. Spread big items like a tent over the fairs you'll use them for.
  • Sales tax and permits. Many places need a seller's permit for in-person sales and expect you to collect sales tax. Check your state or local rules.

Is a craft fair worth it?

Run the numbers before you apply. If you'd need to sell more than you've ever sold in a day just to break even, it's a pass, or a reason to bring more lower-priced items. A fair can still be worth a small profit if it brings custom orders, followers or wholesale contacts, so note those too: a sign-up sheet or a QR code to your shop turns browsers into later buyers. Keep a simple log of each fair (fee, footfall, sales, weather) and after three or four you'll know which ones to rebook.

Craft fair booth display ideas that help you sell

  • Build height. Crates, risers or a small shelf at the back put items at eye level, and shoppers see them from the aisle.
  • Price everything clearly. Many shoppers walk away rather than ask. Group items by price with one sign per group.
  • Show it in use. A hat on a head form, a blanket draped over a chair, a journal open at a finished spread.
  • Keep the front open. Put the table at the back or side so people can step in, and stand rather than sit behind it.
  • A clear sign with your name and a QR code to your shop, so browsers can buy later.
  • Light it. Indoor halls are often dim; a battery lamp or string lights make colours pop.

How to raise your profit at a fair

  • Price from costs, not from the stall next to you. Our pricing guide for handmade items has a calculator with a craft fair option.
  • Have small add-ons at the till. Ornaments, bookmarks, stickers or coasters lift the average sale.
  • Use round prices. $20, not $19.50: quicker change and a smaller float.
  • Bring enough stock. Two to three times what you expect to sell, in a spread of prices. Our craft fair checklist works out the stock and cash float.

Questions crafters ask

What should I record after a craft fair?

The fee and other costs, hours, a rough visitor count, the weather, items sold, takings, how much came by card, the best seller and any custom orders or sign-ups. After three or four fairs you can see which ones are worth booking again.

How do I calculate break-even at a craft fair?

Add up the booth fee and every other cost of the day (travel, food, extras), then divide by what you keep per item after materials, packaging and card fees. With $140 of costs and about $13 kept per $20 item, you need 11 sales to break even.

How much should I sell at a craft fair compared with the booth fee?

A common rule of thumb is sales of at least three times the booth fee: $450 of sales for a $150 booth. Below that, the fair rarely pays for your time.

How much do craft fair booths cost?

Roughly $25 to $75 for small community fairs, $75 to $200 for regional fairs, $100 to $400 for holiday markets and $200 to $500 or more for juried shows, according to TheCraftMap's 2026 guide.

How much do card readers charge at craft fairs?

In the US, phone card readers such as Square charge around 2.6% plus 15 cents per in-person payment. On a $20 sale that's about 67 cents.

Is a craft fair worth it if I only break even?

Sometimes. Count custom orders, new followers and wholesale contacts too. But if a fair only breaks even, your time has been unpaid, so try a cheaper fair, higher prices or more small add-on items.

Do I need to charge sales tax at a craft fair?

In many US states and other countries you do, and you may need a seller's permit for the event. Check your state or local rules; the calculator leaves tax out of your takings.
