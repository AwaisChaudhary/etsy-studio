Yarn substitution calculator

Swapping the yarn in a pattern? Find out how many balls of your yarn to buy, check it's a similar thickness, and see what happens if you hold two strands together.

Quick answer: multiply the pattern's number of balls by the length of one ball to get the total length. Divide that by the length of one ball of your yarn, then round up. A pattern asking for 10 balls of 120 m needs 1,200 m, so with 75 m balls you buy 16. Keep both lengths in the same unit, stay in the same yarn weight, and swatch before you start.

How do I work out how much substitute yarn I need?

  1. Find the total length the pattern uses: number of balls × length of one ball. Nine skeins of 200 yards is 1,800 yards.
  2. Divide by the length of one ball of your yarn. 1,800 ÷ 109 = 16.5.
  3. Round up. You can't buy half a ball, so that's 17.

Always compare length, not grams. Two 50 g balls can hold very different lengths: a fluffy wool might give 150 m, while a dense cotton of the same thickness gives 100 m. One yard is 0.9144 m and one metre is 1.094 yards, so if one label is in yards and the other in metres, the calculator converts for you.

What should match when I swap a yarn?

  • Yarn weight. Start with the same weight number (DK for DK, worsted for worsted). See the yarn weight chart if a label uses a UK or Australian name.
  • Gauge. Knit or crochet a swatch with the pattern's needle or hook, wash and dry it, and measure. Being a stitch or two out over 10 cm is usually fixable with a different needle or hook size. The gauge converter shows how much a difference changes the finished size.
  • Length per 100 g. Within one weight there's a range. If your yarn has a similar length per gram to the pattern yarn, the thickness is probably close. The calculator compares them if you enter the grams.
  • Fibre. Wool is springy and holds ribbing; cotton and bamboo are heavier, drape more and stretch less. Swapping wool for cotton in a fitted sweater can make it grow and hang. Blankets, shawls and scarves are more forgiving.
  • Texture. A plied, smooth yarn shows stitches and cables clearly. Single-ply, fuzzy, chenille or boucle yarns blur them.
Buy one extra ball. Swatching uses yarn, and your tension may be looser than the designer's. Buying 10 to 15% more, or one extra ball, saves hunting for a matching dye lot later. Check all balls share the same dye lot number on the label.

Can I hold two strands together instead?

Yes, two thinner yarns held together can stand in for one thicker yarn, but you'll need twice the length, and you must swatch, because doubled yarn doesn't always behave like a single strand of the heavier weight. Common rules of thumb:

What if my yarn is a different weight?

It can work, but the pattern's numbers won't. A thicker yarn at the pattern's stitch count makes a bigger piece; a thinner one makes a smaller, denser piece. For a blanket or scarf, that may not matter, and the blanket size calculator or yarn calculator can re-estimate the yarn. For anything that has to fit, match the weight or rework the stitch counts from your own gauge.

Questions crafters ask

How do I calculate how much yarn I need when substituting?

Multiply the pattern's number of balls by the length per ball to get the total length, then divide by the length per ball of your yarn and round up. Use the same unit (yards or metres) for both.

Can I substitute yarn by weight in grams?

Not safely. Two yarns of the same weight in grams can have very different lengths. Always compare length (yards or metres), and use grams only to check the yarns are a similar thickness.

What happens if I use a different yarn weight than the pattern?

Your gauge changes, so the piece comes out bigger or smaller than the pattern says, and the yarn amount changes too. Match the weight, or work out new stitch counts from your swatch.

Is two strands of DK the same as worsted?

Roughly. Two strands of DK held together usually work like a worsted or aran yarn, but swatch first, and remember you'll need twice the length of DK.

How much extra yarn should I buy when substituting?

Round up to the next whole ball, then add about 10 to 15% or one extra ball for swatching and differences in tension. Buy it all from the same dye lot.
