Knitting abbreviations

Every common knitting abbreviation in plain English, with how to work it, the UK names, and a pattern decoder that translates a whole line for you.

Quick answer: the ones you'll see most are k (knit), p (purl), st (stitch), k2tog (knit 2 together, a right-leaning decrease), ssk (slip, slip, knit, a left-leaning decrease), M1 (make one, an increase), yo (yarn over, which makes a stitch and a hole), sl (slip), pm (place marker), rep (repeat) and RS / WS (right side / wrong side). A number after k or p tells you how many: k3 means knit 3.

    Knitting abbreviations list

    Search or pick a group. Each one has what it stands for and, where it helps, how to work it. Your pattern's own notes always win, because a few designers use their own short forms.

    kknit
    Knit the stitch. A number after it means knit that many: k3 = knit 3.
    ppurl
    Purl the stitch. p2 = purl 2.
    st(s)stitch(es)
    One loop on the needle is one stitch.
    St ststockinette stitch
    Knit the right-side rows and purl the wrong-side rows. In the round, knit every round.    		UK: stocking stitch
    rev St streverse stockinette stitch
    Stockinette with the bumpy purl side used as the right side.    		UK: reverse stocking stitch
    g stgarter stitch
    Knit every row when working flat. In the round, knit one round and purl the next.
    ribrib
    Columns of knits and purls, such as k1, p1 or k2, p2. Knit the knits and purl the purls as they face you.
    seed stseed stitch
    k1, p1 that shifts every row, so you knit the purls and purl the knits.    		UK: moss stitch
    tblthrough the back loop
    Work into the back leg of the stitch, which twists it. k1 tbl = knit 1 through the back loop.
    tflthrough the front loop
    Work into the front leg of the stitch, the usual way.
    k1bknit into the stitch below
    Knit into the stitch in the row below the next one on the needle.
    kwise / pwiseknitwise / purlwise
    Put the needle in as if to knit (or purl). Usually used with slipping.
    COcast on
    Put the first stitches on the needle.
    BObind off
    Take the stitches off so they don't unravel.    		UK: cast off
    pupick up (and knit)
    Pull new stitches up through the edge of the piece, for a collar, border or gusset.
    w&twrap and turn
    For short rows: slip the next stitch, move the yarn to the other side, slip the stitch back and turn the work.
    MBmake bobble
    Work several stitches into one stitch, then join them into a bump. The pattern says exactly how.
    incincrease
    Make one or more extra stitches. The pattern or its notes say which method.
    kfbknit into the front and back
    Knit the stitch but leave it on the needle, then knit into its back loop too. Turns 1 stitch into 2.
    pfbpurl into the front and back
    The purl version of kfb. Turns 1 stitch into 2.
    M1 / M1Lmake one (left-leaning)
    Lift the bar between two stitches from front to back with the left needle and knit it through the back loop.
    M1Rmake one right
    Lift the bar between two stitches from back to front and knit it through the front loop. Leans right.
    M1pmake one purlwise
    Lift the bar between two stitches and purl it. L and R versions lean left or right.
    kll / krllifted increase (left / right)
    Knit into the stitch below the one on the needle (or two below for the left one) to add a stitch.
    yoyarn over
    Wrap the yarn over the right needle to make a new stitch with a small hole under it. Used for lace and eyelets.    		UK: yf, yfwd, yon or yrn
    yo2yarn over twice
    Wrap the yarn round the needle twice. The pattern says how to work the extra loop next row.
    decdecrease
    Lose one or more stitches. The pattern or its notes say which method.
    k2togknit 2 together
    Knit two stitches as if they were one. Leans right. Loses 1 stitch.
    p2togpurl 2 together
    Purl two stitches as one. Loses 1 stitch.
    k3togknit 3 together
    Knit three stitches as one. Leans right. Loses 2 stitches.
    sskslip, slip, knit
    Slip two stitches knitwise one at a time, put the left needle into their fronts and knit them together through the back loops. Leans left. Loses 1 stitch.
    sspslip, slip, purl
    Slip two stitches knitwise one at a time, put them back and purl them together through the back loops. Loses 1 stitch.
    ssskslip, slip, slip, knit
    Like ssk but with three stitches. Leans left. Loses 2 stitches.
    skpslip 1, knit 1, pass the slipped stitch over
    Slip 1 knitwise, knit 1, then lift the slipped stitch over the knitted one. Leans left. Loses 1 stitch.    		UK patterns often write skpo
    sk2pslip 1, knit 2 together, pass the slipped stitch over
    A left-leaning double decrease. Loses 2 stitches.
    s2kp / cddcentred double decrease
    Slip 2 together knitwise, knit 1, pass both slipped stitches over. The middle stitch stays on top. Loses 2 stitches.
    pssopass the slipped stitch over
    Lift a stitch you slipped earlier over the one you just worked and off the needle.
    p2ssopass 2 slipped stitches over
    Lift the two slipped stitches over the one you just worked.
    pnsopass the next stitch over
    Lift the next stitch over and off the needle.
    togtogether
    Work the stitches as one, as in k2tog.
    slslip
    Move a stitch to the right needle without working it. Most patterns mean purlwise unless they say otherwise or it's part of a decrease.
    sl1k / sl1pslip 1 knitwise / purlwise
    Slip one stitch, putting the needle in as if to knit (k) or purl (p).
    sl stslip stitch
    A stitch moved without being worked.    		Canada: ss
    wyib / wyifwith yarn in back / in front
    Keep the yarn behind (or in front of) the work while you slip.
    yb / yfyarn to the back / yarn forward
    Move the yarn between the needles to the back or the front. In UK lace, yfwd between two knit stitches makes a yarn over.
    yon / yrnyarn over (UK style)
    UK names for a yarn over, depending on whether the stitches either side are knits or purls.    		US: yo
    cncable needle
    A short needle that holds stitches out of the way while you cross a cable.
    C4F / C4Bcable 4 front / back
    Slip 2 stitches to a cable needle and hold it in front (or back), knit 2, then knit the 2 from the cable needle. Front leans left, back leans right. C6F, C8B and so on work the same way with more stitches.
    LC / RCleft cross / right cross
    A cable crossing to the left (cable needle held in front) or to the right (held in back). Numbers like 2/2 LC give how many stitches cross.
    LT / RTleft twist / right twist
    A tiny two-stitch cross, often worked without a cable needle.    		Some UK patterns: FT / BT
    FC / BCfront cross / back cross
    Older names for a cable held in front or behind.
    pmplace marker
    Put a stitch marker on the needle here.
    smslip marker
    Move the marker from the left needle to the right.
    rmremove marker
    Take the marker off.
    mmarker (or metres)
    A stitch marker. In measurements, m means metres.
    BORbeginning of round
    Where each round starts, usually shown with a different marker.
    MC / CCmain colour / contrast colour
    The main yarn, and the second colour used for stripes or colourwork.
    CA / CBcolour A / colour B
    Names for the yarns in a pattern with several colours.
    RS / WSright side / wrong side
    The side that shows when it's finished, and the side that faces inwards.
    rnd(s)round(s)
    One trip round the work when knitting in the round.
    reprepeat
    Work the instructions again.
    remremaining
    The stitches left, often given after a decrease row: 64 sts rem.
    begbeginning
    The start of a row, round or pattern.
    contcontinue
    Keep working as you have been.
    altalternate
    Every other one: on alt rows means every second row.
    follfollowing
    The next one, as in the foll 4 rows.
    patt / patpattern
    The stitch pattern, or the pattern as written.
    prevprevious
    The one before.
    betbetween
    approxapproximately
    inclincluding
    LH / RHleft-hand / right-hand
    Usually the left or right needle.
    lp(s)loop(s)
    dpn(s)double-pointed needle(s)
    Short needles with a point at each end, used for small rounds like socks and hat crowns.

    Check the pattern's notes: some use it for every other row, some for end of row.

    Repeat the instructions after the asterisk as many times as the pattern says, or to the end.

    yd / myards / metres

    What's the difference between k2tog and ssk?

    Both turn two stitches into one. k2tog leans to the right and ssk leans to the left. Patterns pair them so shaping looks tidy on both edges, such as the sides of a hat crown, a raglan line or a sock toe: ssk at the start of the shaping and k2tog at the end, or the other way round, as the pattern says. If you swap them nothing goes wrong with the count, the line just leans the other way.

    What does * mean in a knitting pattern?

    An asterisk marks the start of a repeat. "*k2, p2; rep from * to end" means knit 2, purl 2, and keep doing that until the row ends. Two asterisks (**) mark a second, longer repeat, and square brackets or round brackets mean "work this group the number of times written after it": [k1, p1] 4 times.

    How do I read a knitting pattern for the first time?

    1. Read the notes at the top first. They list the abbreviations, special stitches and whether the piece is worked flat or in the round.
    2. Check the gauge and knit a swatch. Our gauge converter shows what happens if yours is different.
    3. Read one row at a time and say it out loud in plain words, or paste it into the decoder above.
    4. Check the stitch count at the end of each shaping row. Most patterns give it in brackets or after a dash.
    5. Keep your place. The row counter keeps count and repeat numbers for you.
    6. Dropped one? Don't panic or rip back yet: our guide to fixing a dropped stitch shows how to ladder it back up with a crochet hook.

    Questions crafters ask

    What does k2tog mean in knitting?

    Knit 2 together: put the needle into the next two stitches as if to knit and knit them as one. It's a decrease that leans to the right and loses one stitch.

    What does ssk mean in knitting?

    Slip, slip, knit: slip two stitches knitwise one at a time, then knit them together through the back loops. It's a decrease that leans to the left.

    What does M1 mean in knitting?

    Make one: an increase made by lifting the bar of yarn between two stitches onto the left needle and knitting it through the back loop, so it doesn't leave a hole. M1L leans left and M1R leans right.

    What does yo mean in knitting?

    Yarn over: wrap the yarn over the right needle before the next stitch. It adds a stitch and leaves a small hole, which is how lace and eyelets are made. UK patterns may call it yfwd, yon or yrn.

    What does pm and sm mean in knitting?

    pm means place a stitch marker on your needle, and sm means slip the marker from the left needle to the right when you reach it.

    Are knitting abbreviations different in the UK?

    Mostly no. The big differences are words: cast off instead of bind off, tension instead of gauge, stocking stitch instead of stockinette, moss stitch instead of seed stitch, and yfwd, yon or yrn for a yarn over.

    What does RS and WS mean in knitting?

    RS is the right side, the side that shows on the finished piece. WS is the wrong side, which faces inwards.
