Junk journal page ideas

42 junk journal page ideas sorted by theme, six spread layouts drawn out, and a spread generator that picks a background, a focal piece, something interactive, an embellishment and a prompt for you, so you never face a blank page again.

Quick answer: a junk journal page comes together in five layers: a background (book page, map, tea-stained paper, a flattened paper bag), a focal piece (a photo, a postcard, a big label), something interactive (a pocket, tag, flap or tuck spot), a few embellishments (washi, stamps, stitching, a button) and a few words. Pick one idea from each layer and the page is done. Stuck? Press the shuffle button below for a ready-made spread, or try one of the 42 ideas further down: memories, seasons, nature, vintage collage, writing prompts, interactive pages and pages made only from things you already have at home.

Six junk journal spread layouts

42 junk journal page ideas

Memories

  1. Ticket stub timeline. Glue down tickets from a year in date order and write one line under each.
  2. A day in my life. Times down the side, one small sketch or note for each hour.
  3. Then and now. An old photo and a new one side by side, with what changed.
  4. Recipe page. A family recipe in the cook's handwriting, a splash of tea stain and a fold-out for the story.
  5. Letter to future me. Seal it in an envelope pocket with an open-on date.
  6. Favourite things list. Book, song, smell, place, food: one tag each.

Seasons and holidays

  1. Winter window. A paper window frame with snowflakes behind it and a cosy list on the sill.
  2. Christmas card wall. Cut fronts from last year's cards into a grid. Add who sent each one.
  3. Advent pocket page. 24 tiny tags in pockets, one line written each December day.
  4. New Year word. Your word for the year in big letters cut from magazines, with three ways to live it.
  5. Spring seed packet page. Old seed packets as pockets for garden notes.
  6. Summer postcard. Make your own postcard from a scrap of card and write it to a friend you miss.

Nature

  1. Pressed leaf page. Flatten leaves in a heavy book for a week, tape them down and label them.
  2. Weather log. One small square a day coloured for the weather, like a temperature blanket on paper.
  3. Moon phases strip. Eight circles shaded for the moon's phases down the edge.
  4. Botanical label page. Vintage-style labels with plant names, real or made up.
  5. Bird watch tally. A tally of birds seen from your window this month.
  6. Tea-stained sky. A tea-dyed page with a cut paper landscape at the bottom.

Collage and vintage

  1. Old book page base. A page from a damaged book as the background, words picked out with a highlighter.
  2. Envelope layers. Three envelopes in different sizes stacked like steps, each one a pocket.
  3. Ephemera cluster. One corner packed with labels, stamps and tickets, the rest left calm.
  4. Lace and doily page. A doily or lace strip across the middle with a photo on top.
  5. Map background. An old map or atlas page with a dashed line to somewhere you want to go.
  6. Stamp collection grid. Real or printed postage stamps in a 3 x 3 grid.

Writing and prompts

  1. Gratitude tags. Three tags: a person, a thing and a moment you're glad of this week.
  2. Overheard quotes. Funny or kind things people said, typed on strips like fortune cookie papers.
  3. Blackout poem. Black out words on a book page until a short poem is left.
  4. Lists of five. Five things you can see, hear, smell, touch and taste right now.
  5. Question of the month. One question at the top, a different answer each week.
  6. Dream page. Last night's dream written fast, before you forget it, with doodles.

Interactive pages

  1. Pull-tab reveal. A tab that slides out from behind a photo to show the date and story.
  2. Mini book inside. A tiny folded zine glued in as a page within the page.
  3. Belly band bundle. Cards held under a band so they come out like a deck.
  4. Waterfall cards. Overlapping cards that drop down when you pull a tab.
  5. Envelope of extras. A sealed envelope full of loose ephemera to use another day.
  6. Tea bag pocket. A dried, emptied tea bag as a tiny see-through pocket.

Free from what's at home

  1. Packaging prints. Cut pretty bits from food boxes and tea packets.
  2. Receipt poetry. A long receipt trimmed into a strip with the shop's name as a title.
  3. Junk mail collage. Colours and words from flyers, sorted into a rainbow.
  4. Paper bag page. A flattened brown bag as the page, its fold as a pocket.
  5. Coffee stain circles. Ring stains from a mug as frames for tiny drawings.
  6. Kids' art page. A child's drawing trimmed and framed with washi tape.

Christmas junk journal ideas

December is a lovely time to start a junk journal: there's paper everywhere, from cards to wrapping to food packaging. Here are twelve Christmas page ideas and three free templates to trace or print and cut out (our own designs; dashed lines are the cut lines). Print on card or kraft paper and scale the drawing to the size you want.

  1. Advent tag strip. Number 24 small tags and add one line or a tiny find to each December day.
  2. Mitten pocket. Cut the mitten template from a festive paper, glue three sides and tuck a wish list inside.
  3. Christmas card mosaic. Trim the fronts of last year's cards into squares and grid them across a spread.
  4. Recipe of the season. Write out a family bake on a recipe card, with a pocket for the crumb-stained original.
  5. Gift log. A simple table: who, what, made or bought. Handy for next year too.
  6. North Pole ticket. A ticket for a Christmas outing, real or pretend, with the date and who came.
  7. Wrapping paper background. Flatten leftover wrapping paper or tissue as the base layer.
  8. Ornament page. Draw or cut paper baubles and write a memory in each one.
  9. Twelve days list. Twelve tags or flaps, one small joy per day from Christmas to Twelfth Night.
  10. Winter walk. Pressed holly or a pine sprig sealed under a strip of packing tape, with where you found it.
  11. Letter to Santa. A sealed envelope pocket with a letter, or a grown-up version: what you'd wish for this year.
  12. Year in review. A spread for the end of December: best moment, hardest moment, one word for next year.

How to start a junk journal page

  1. Choose a background first. It sets the colours for everything else, so pick it before anything gets glued.
  2. Lay it all out dry. Move pieces around before gluing. Take a quick photo when you like it, so you can put it back.
  3. Glue the biggest pieces first, then the smaller ones on top. Leave the edges of a few pieces loose to make tuck spots.
  4. Add one interactive piece: a pocket, a tag, a flap. It gives the page a reason to be picked up.
  5. Finish with words. A date, a quote, a few lines from a journal prompt. It's what makes the page yours.

What if my page looks too busy?

Leave one area empty, or cover it with a single calm paper. Stick to three main colours, and repeat each one at least twice across the spread so the eye moves round it. Odd numbers (three tags, five stamps) look more relaxed than pairs. If a page really isn't working, glue a plain sheet over the worst part and call it a new background: nobody else will know.

Where to find things for your pages

Most of it is free: envelopes, packaging, receipts, old books, tea bags. See our 62 ephemera ideas and the supply checklist, give white paper an old look by tea staining it, and work out pages and binding with the signature calculator. New to all this? Start with junk journaling for beginners.

Questions crafters ask

What do you put on a junk journal page?

A background paper, one focal piece like a photo or postcard, something interactive like a pocket or tag, a few embellishments and a few words.

How do I fill my junk journal?

Do one spread at a time: pick a background, lay everything out before gluing, add one pocket or tag, then write a date or a few lines. Don't try to fill every gap.

What should be on the first page of a junk journal?

Something that says whose journal it is: your name, the year or a word for the journal, with a favourite photo or paper. Many people leave it until last.

How do you make a junk journal page look vintage?

Tea-stain the paper, ink the edges with brown ink, and use old book pages, stamps, lace and labels. Keep the colours warm and muted.

Can you junk journal without buying supplies?

Yes. Envelopes, food packaging, receipts, junk mail, paper bags, tea bags and old books are all free and make great pages.

What can I put in a Christmas junk journal?

Christmas cards, wrapping paper, gift tags, receipts from presents, recipe cards, ticket stubs, pressed holly or pine, photos and a gift list. Most of it is free from the season.

How do you make a Christmas junk journal?

Fold a few signatures from festive paper and old cards, bind them into a cover made from a cereal box or cardstock, then add a page or two each day of December.

How many pages should a junk journal have?

Most handmade junk journals have 3 to 6 signatures of 4 to 6 folded sheets each. Make it smaller than you think: a thin finished journal beats a fat empty one.
