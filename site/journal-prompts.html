How do you use a junk journal prompt?

  1. Pick one prompt and read it twice. Don't wait for the perfect idea.
  2. Choose a background: a book page, a tea-stained sheet or a scrap of packaging.
  3. Add the focal piece: the ephemera the prompt suggests, or the closest thing in your stash.
  4. Write a few lines on a tag, card or envelope. Short is fine.
  5. Finish with one small detail: washi tape, a stamp, a stitched edge or a date.

Using prompts without getting stuck

Treat a prompt as a starting point, not a rule. If "a ticket stub" isn't in your stash, use anything with printed numbers on it. If the palette doesn't suit the theme, keep just one colour from it.

A good rhythm for a two-page spread: lay down a background paper, add one big focal piece, tuck in something interactive (a pocket, flap or tag), and finish with a few words. New to all this? Start with our junk journaling guide for beginners, and age your own background papers with our guide to tea staining paper.

How often should you journal?

As often as you enjoy it. Some people make one spread a week, others one page a day through December. A prompt a day works well for a month-long project like December Daily, because each page stays small and you don't have to decide what to make.

What if I don't like writing?

Skip the writing prompt and answer it with a picture, a list of three words or a single date. A junk journal is a scrapbook as much as a diary, and plenty of pages have no writing at all.

Questions crafters ask

What do you put in a junk journal?

Anything flat and paper-like that you'd normally throw away: receipts, tickets, envelopes, tea bags, packaging, book pages, plus photos, washi tape, fabric and your own writing.

How do I get ideas for junk journal pages?

Pick one theme and one thing to glue in, then build around them. The shuffle above does this for you, and you can lock what you like.

What are some winter or Christmas junk journal prompts?

Try the first frost of the year, your favourite Christmas tradition, cards that came through the door, or a recipe that means Christmas to you. Glue in a card envelope, a gift tag or a strip of wrapping paper.

What should I write in a junk journal?

Short answers to simple questions work best: what made you smile today, a smell that takes you back, a story someone in your family always tells. A list or a few words on a tag is enough.

How often should I use journal prompts?

As often as you like. One spread a week is a relaxed pace; a prompt a day suits month-long projects like December Daily.
