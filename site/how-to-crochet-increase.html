How to increase in crochet

How to add stitches in crochet: the single crochet increase, increases in taller stitches, the invisible increase and edge increases, each with our own chart diagram. Plus a writer that spaces your increases evenly across any round or row.

Quick answer: to increase in crochet, work two stitches into the same stitch. For a single crochet increase (written inc or 2 sc in next st), work one single crochet, then put your hook back into that same stitch and work another one. One stitch becomes two, so the row grows by one. To increase evenly, divide your stitches by the number of increases: going from 24 to 30 stitches is 6 increases, so (sc 3, inc) 6 times. In UK patterns single crochet is called double crochet (dc), but the move is the same.

How to do a single crochet increase, step by step

  1. Work a single crochet as normal: insert the hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over and pull through both loops.
  2. Go back into the same stitch. Insert the hook into the exact stitch you just worked, not the next one.
  3. Work a second single crochet there. You now have two stitches sitting on one stitch of the row below.
  4. Carry on with the next stitch as the pattern says. Count at the end of the row or round: it should have grown by one for every increase.

Stitch markers help a lot here: put one in the first stitch of each round, and in a tricky pattern clip one onto each increase so you can count them.

How to increase with taller stitches

Half double, double and treble crochet increase in exactly the same way: work the stitch, then work another one into the same place. With taller stitches the pair fans out and leaves a small gap at the top, which is normal and blocks out. Some patterns write it as 2 dc in next st; a V-stitch is the same idea with a chain between, (dc, ch 1, dc) in one stitch, for a lacier look.

The invisible increase

A normal sc inc can leave a little bump or a hole, which shows on amigurumi. For a neater one, work the first single crochet into the front loop only, then the second into both loops of the same stitch. The pair sits flatter and the gap closes. It's worth learning if you make toys; for blankets the normal increase is fine.

How to increase evenly in a round

Flat circles grow by the same number of stitches every round: 6 for single crochet, about 8 for half double and 12 for double crochet. Round 2 is "inc in every stitch", round 3 is (sc 1, inc), round 4 is (sc 2, inc) and so on. That puts the increases in the same place every round, which slowly turns a circle into a hexagon. To keep it round, stagger the increases: on every other round start with half of the plain stitches, for example sc 1, inc, (sc 2, inc) 5 times, sc 1 instead of (sc 2, inc) 6 times. The writer above gives you both versions. For the full chart, see the crochet circle calculator.

How to increase at the end of a row

Work 2 stitches into the first stitch, 2 into the last, or both, to widen a piece at the edges, as in a triangle shawl or a sleeve. For a neater edge, put the increase one stitch in from the edge instead. To add several stitches at once at the end of a row, chain the number you need (plus the turning chain) and work back along the chains.

Why is my crochet getting wider (or not)?

  • Piece slowly gets wider without meaning to: you're working 2 stitches into the first or last stitch by accident, often into the turning chain. Count each row.
  • Circle cups like a bowl: not enough increases, or your stitches are tight. Check the count, or go up a hook size.
  • Circle ruffles like a frill: too many increases. Count the last round and rip back to where it went wrong.
  • Need to go smaller? See how to decrease in crochet, the mirror of this page.

Questions crafters ask

How do you increase in crochet?

Work two stitches into the same stitch. For a single crochet increase, work one sc, then insert the hook into the same stitch again and work a second sc.

What does inc mean in crochet?

Increase: work 2 stitches in the same stitch, usually single crochet in amigurumi patterns. Some patterns write it as 2 sc in next st.

How do you increase evenly in crochet?

Divide the stitches you have by the number of increases. From 24 to 30 is 6 increases, and 24 divided by 6 is 4, so work (sc 3, inc) 6 times.

What is an invisible increase in crochet?

Work the first single crochet in the front loop only and the second in both loops of the same stitch. It sits flatter than a normal increase and hides the gap.

How many stitches do you increase each round in a crochet circle?

Usually 6 for single crochet, about 8 for half double and 12 for double crochet, spread evenly around the round.

Why is my crochet circle turning into a hexagon?

The increases are stacking in the same place every round. Stagger them by starting every other round with half the plain stitches before the first increase.

Does an increase count as one stitch or two?

Two. An increase makes two stitches out of one, so each increase adds one stitch to your count.
