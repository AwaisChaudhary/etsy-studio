How to knit and purl: the two stitches every pattern uses

Every knitting pattern, from a garter scarf to a cabled sweater, is made of knit and purl stitches. Learn both in four steps each, with our own drawings, then use the pattern maker to write out the rows for stockinette, garter, rib or seed stitch.

Quick answer: to knit, keep the yarn at the back, put the right needle into the stitch from front to back, wrap the yarn, pull the new loop through to the front and slide the old stitch off. To purl, bring the yarn to the front, go into the stitch from back to front, wrap, push the loop through to the back and slide off. Knitting every row makes garter stitch; knitting one row and purling the next makes stockinette (smooth Vs on the front).

How to knit stitch: step by step

Cast on about 20 stitches first (see how to cast on), using a smooth, light-coloured worsted yarn and 5 mm (US 8) needles so you can see each stitch.

Our drawings, right-handed, English (throwing) style. The dashed yarn is behind the work. Continental knitters hold the yarn in the left hand and pick it with the needle instead of wrapping, but the needle goes into the stitch the same way.

How to purl stitch: step by step

The purl is the knit stitch done from the other side. The two differences: the yarn is in front, and the right needle goes in from the back of the stitch to the front.

How do I tell a knit stitch from a purl stitch?

A knit stitch looks like a little V. A purl stitch looks like a little bump, like a bead on a string. They're the same stitch seen from opposite sides: the back of every knit is a purl. That's why "knit the knits and purl the purls" works for ribbing: look at the stitch, and if it's a V, knit it; if it's a bump, purl it.

Why did I get extra stitches when switching from knit to purl?

When you change between knit and purl in the same row, the yarn has to move between the needle tips: to the front before a purl, to the back before a knit. If you take it over the needle instead, it wraps the needle and makes an extra loop (a yarn over) and a hole. Count your stitches at the end of each row while you learn, and use a row counter so you know which row you're on.

Why is my knitting so tight (or so loose)?

Usually you're pulling the yarn after every stitch, or working on the very tips of the needles. Make each new stitch on the thick part of the right needle, not the tip, and let the yarn run through your fingers without tugging. Loose, uneven purls are common too (some knitters call it "rowing out"); wrapping the purl a little more snugly usually fixes it. Your stitches even out with practice, so knit a 20-stitch swatch before a project and check your gauge.

Which is easier, knit or purl?

Most beginners find the knit stitch easier, which is why a garter stitch scarf (knit every row) is the classic first project. Once you're comfortable, add the purl and try stockinette, then ribbing for a hat brim. Both stitches take the same four moves: in, wrap, through, off.

What does "right side" and "wrong side" mean?

The right side (RS) is the side that will show. In stockinette it's the smooth knit side. The wrong side (WS) is the back, with the purl bumps. When you knit flat you turn the work at the end of each row, so you work RS rows and WS rows in turn; when you knit in the round the right side always faces you, so stockinette is just knit every round.

Questions crafters ask

How do you knit a stitch step by step?

Yarn at the back. Put the right needle into the first stitch from front to back, wrap the yarn around the right needle anticlockwise, pull the new loop through to the front, and slide the old stitch off the left needle.

How do you purl step by step?

Yarn in front. Put the right needle into the first stitch from back to front (right to left), wrap the yarn anticlockwise, push the new loop through to the back, and slide the old stitch off.

What is the difference between knit and purl?

They're the same stitch from opposite sides. A knit makes a V on the side facing you and a purl makes a bump. Knit goes in front to back with the yarn behind; purl goes in back to front with the yarn in front.

What happens if you only knit every row?

You get garter stitch: ridged on both sides, flat and a little stretchy. Knit one row and purl the next for stockinette.

Why does my stockinette curl?

Stockinette always curls at the edges because the knit and purl sides pull differently. Add a garter, seed or rib border a few stitches wide, or block it; a border is the reliable fix.

What does k1, p1 mean?

Knit 1 stitch, purl 1 stitch, then repeat. Bring the yarn between the needles to the front before each purl and to the back before each knit.
