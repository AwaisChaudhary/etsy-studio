How to increase in knitting

Six ways to add a stitch, from knit front and back to make 1 left and right, with our own diagrams, a picker that tells you which increase to use, and how to space increases evenly.

Quick answer: the easiest increase is knit front and back (kfb): knit the stitch as normal but leave it on the left needle, then knit into its back loop and slide it off. One stitch becomes two. For a near-invisible increase, use make 1 (M1L or M1R): pick up the horizontal bar between two stitches with the left needle and knit it twisted so no hole forms. A yarn over also adds a stitch, but leaves a hole on purpose. If your pattern just says "M1", it usually means M1L.

Knitting increases at a glance

How to knit front and back (kfb)

  1. Insert the right needle into the next stitch knitwise and knit it, but don't slide the old stitch off the left needle.
  2. Swing the right needle behind and insert it into the back loop of that same stitch, from right to left.
  3. Wrap the yarn and pull the loop through, then slip the old stitch off. You now have two stitches where there was one.

The second stitch has a little horizontal bar at its base, like a purl bump. In garter stitch it hides; in stocking stitch it shows slightly, which is fine at edges. The purl version is pfb.

How to make 1 left (M1L)

  1. Look between the last stitch on your right needle and the next one on the left: there's a horizontal strand, the bar.
  2. With the left needle, pick up the bar from front to back.
  3. Knit it through the back loop (insert the right needle into the back of the lifted strand). The twist closes the hole and the new stitch leans left.

How to make 1 right (M1R)

  1. With the left needle, pick up the bar from back to front.
  2. Knit it through the front loop. This twists it the other way, so the stitch leans right.

M1L and M1R are a mirror pair. Use one on each side of a centre line (a raglan seam, a sleeve edge) and the increases look matched. If the bar feels too tight to knit, you may have picked it up the wrong way round: try the other one.

How to do a yarn over increase

Bring the yarn to the front between the needles, then knit the next stitch: the yarn goes over the right needle and makes an extra loop. On the next row, work that loop as a normal stitch and it leaves a neat eyelet hole. That's what lace is built from, and it's a pretty choice for raglan lines or a baby blanket edge. Want no hole? Knit the yarn over through the back loop on the next row to twist it shut.

Lifted increases (RLI and LLI)

Right lifted increase (RLI): with the right needle, lift the right leg of the stitch in the row below the next stitch onto the left needle and knit it, then knit the stitch itself. Left lifted increase (LLI): after knitting a stitch, use the left needle to lift the left leg of the stitch two rows below the one you just made, and knit it. Lifted increases pull a little yarn from the row below, so they're nearly invisible in stocking stitch. Don't stack them on top of each other every row, or the fabric puckers.

Backward loop increase

Wrap the yarn around your thumb, slip the loop onto the right needle and tighten it. It's the same as a backward loop cast on, worked mid-row. It's fast but can look loose and a little holey; it's handy for adding stitches at the very end of a row, or for underarm stitches on a top-down sweater.

How to increase evenly across a row

Divide your stitches by the number of increases to find how often to add one. For 80 stitches and 8 increases, that's one increase every 10 stitches: knit 5, M1, then (knit 10, M1) 7 more times, knit 5. Starting with half a section keeps the increases away from the edges. Our increase evenly calculator writes this out for any numbers.

Common increase problems

  • A hole where you didn't want one: an M1 that wasn't twisted, or an accidental yarn over. Lift the bar the right way and knit through the back (M1L) or front (M1R).
  • Too many stitches: a stray yarn over is the usual cause, often when the yarn sits in front before a knit stitch. Count after every increase row.
  • Increase looks lumpy: try an M1 or lifted increase instead of kfb in stocking stitch.
  • Dropped one? See how to fix a dropped stitch.

Questions crafters ask

What is the easiest way to increase in knitting?

Knit front and back (kfb): knit the stitch but leave it on the needle, knit into its back loop as well, then slip it off. One stitch becomes two.

What does M1 mean in knitting?

Make 1: pick up the bar between two stitches and knit it twisted to add a stitch. Plain M1 usually means M1L, which leans left. M1R leans right.

What is the difference between M1L and M1R?

M1L picks up the bar from front to back and knits it through the back loop, leaning left. M1R picks it up from back to front and knits it through the front loop, leaning right.

Which knitting increase is invisible?

Lifted increases and M1 are the least visible in stocking stitch. Kfb leaves a small bump and a yarn over leaves a hole.

Why does my M1 leave a hole?

The lifted bar wasn't twisted. Knit an M1L through the back loop and an M1R through the front loop and the hole closes.

How do you increase evenly across a row in knitting?

Divide the stitch count by the number of increases to get the spacing, then put half a space at each end. For 80 stitches and 8 increases: k5, M1, (k10, M1) 7 times, k5.

Does a yarn over add a stitch?

Yes. A yarn over makes an extra loop on the needle that you work as a stitch on the next row, leaving a decorative hole.
