Macrame knots for beginners

The ten basic macrame knots, each drawn so you can see where the cords go, with plain step-by-step instructions. Then tell the project finder what you want to make and it lists the knots you need, in order, with the cord to buy and how long to cut it.

Quick answer: start with four knots. The lark's head (or reverse lark's head) mounts cords on a dowel or ring; the square knot is the knot most patterns are built from; the half knot spiral is half a square knot repeated; and the double half hitch makes straight or diagonal lines. Add the gathering knot to finish a plant hanger and the overhand knot for fringe and beads, and you can make most beginner patterns. Practise on 4 mm (about 3/16 in) single-strand or 3-ply cotton cord: it's soft, easy to undo and shows the knots clearly.

The 10 basic macrame knots

Pink and green are the working cords (the ones you tie with). Tan is the filler or holding cord that the knot goes round.

How to tie a lark's head knot

  1. Fold a cord in half to make a loop at the middle.
  2. Lay the loop over the front of the dowel (or ring) and fold it down behind.
  3. Pull both cord ends through the loop from front to back, then pull them down to tighten.

For a reverse lark's head, lay the loop over the back of the dowel and fold it down in front, then pull the ends through. The bar sits behind, so the front looks like two neat bumps. Both use the same amount of cord.

How to tie a square knot

You need four cords: an outer cord on each side (the working cords) and two in the middle (the fillers). Pull the fillers straight and keep a little tension on them.

  1. Left over: take the left cord across the front of the fillers, making a shape like the number 4.
  2. Right over, under, through: lay the right cord over the left cord's tail, take it behind the fillers and bring it up through the loop on the left. Pull both working cords out to the sides to tighten. That's a half knot.
  3. Mirror it: now take the right cord across the front of the fillers (a backwards 4), lay the left cord over its tail, go behind the fillers and up through the loop on the right. Tighten.

Done right, the bump (the little loop at the side) sits on the side you started from. If your sinnet starts twisting, you've tied the same half twice: start the next knot from the other side. A row of square knots that move across by two cords on every row is called alternating square knots, the net look on most wall hangings.

How to make a half knot spiral

Tie the first half of a square knot and then repeat it, always starting from the same side. After four to six knots the sinnet starts to turn. Let it: flip the working cords over each other as they swap sides and keep going. Starting from the left twists one way and starting from the right twists the other way, so keep to one side for a whole spiral.

How to tie half hitches and double half hitches

  1. Hold one cord (the holding cord) straight across the other cords, left to right, a little below where you want the line.
  2. Take the first cord under it, bring it up over the holding cord and down through the loop it made. That's a half hitch.
  3. Do it again with the same cord, to the right of the first loop. Two half hitches make a double half hitch.
  4. Move to the next cord and repeat all the way across. Keep the holding cord tight and push each knot up close to the last.

For a diagonal line, hold the holding cord at a slant instead of straight. To go back the other way, use the last cord as the new holding cord and tie right to left.

How to tie a gathering knot

  1. Cut a separate cord about 3 ft (90 cm) long. Make a U shape with it and lay it along the bundle, loop pointing down, one short end sticking up.
  2. Wrap the long end round the bundle and the U from the top down, tight and neat, 6 to 10 times.
  3. Push the end of the wrapping cord through the loop at the bottom.
  4. Pull the short end at the top until the loop and end disappear under the wraps. Trim both ends close and tuck them in.

Which cord to use

  • Single-strand (single twist) cotton: soft, combs out into a fluffy fringe, best for wall hangings and feathers. Easy on the hands.
  • 3-ply cotton rope: stronger and more forgiving, the best choice for plant hangers that hold weight. Unravels into wavy fringe.
  • Braided cord: doesn't fray and keeps its shape. Good for keychains and outdoor pieces, but the knots are harder to undo.
  • Thickness: 3 mm for keychains, ornaments and coasters, 4 to 5 mm for most wall hangings and plant hangers, 6 mm and up for big statement pieces.

Tips for neat knots

  • Hang your work at eye height: a dowel hung from a clothes rail, a door handle or an S hook makes tying much easier than working flat.
  • Same tension every time: pull each knot to the same tightness. Loose and tight knots side by side are the main thing that makes macrame look uneven.
  • Measure the gaps: leave the same space between rows. A ruler or a strip of card cut to the gap helps.
  • Cut long: running out of cord halfway is the classic beginner mistake. Cut a test cord first and see how much a few knots use.
  • Tape the ends of 3-ply cord while you work so it doesn't unravel before you want the fringe.

Questions crafters ask

What are the basic macrame knots?

The lark's head and reverse lark's head (to mount cords), the square knot, the half knot spiral, the half hitch and double half hitch, the gathering knot and the overhand knot.

What is the easiest macrame knot for beginners?

The lark's head is the easiest and comes first in almost every project. The square knot is the one to practise next, because most patterns are built from it.

How do you tie a macrame square knot?

Take the left cord over the fillers in a 4 shape, the right cord over it, behind the fillers and up through the loop. Then do the mirror image starting from the right.

Why is my macrame square knot twisting?

You're tying the same half knot twice. Alternate the side you start from on every half knot. A twist on purpose is called a half knot spiral.

What size macrame cord is best for beginners?

4 mm single-strand or 3-ply cotton cord. It's thick enough to see the knots, soft on the hands and easy to undo when you make a mistake.

What is the difference between a lark's head and a reverse lark's head?

They're the same knot from opposite sides. The lark's head shows a bar across the front; the reverse lark's head hides it at the back and shows two bumps.
