How to wind a ball of yarn by hand (centre pull)

A centre-pull ball sits still in your project bag while the yarn feeds from the middle. You can wind one in about 10 minutes with nothing but your hand. Every step is drawn below, plus how to open a hank without tangles and a calculator for splitting a skein into equal balls on a kitchen scale.

Quick answer: to wind a centre-pull ball by hand, hold a 6 in tail under your thumb, wrap the yarn in a figure 8 round two fingers about 20 times, fold that bundle in half to make a core, then wrap diagonally over the core and your thumb, turning the ball a little every few wraps. Keep it loose. When you're done, tuck the end in and pull the tail from the middle. A 100 g skein takes about 10 to 15 minutes.

How to wind a centre-pull ball, step by step

Do you need to wind yarn into a ball?

Yarn sold as a ball or a skein (the long oval shape most acrylic comes in) is ready to use: find the end in the middle and pull. Yarn sold as a hank, a big loop twisted into a figure 8, must be wound first, or it will tangle into a nest the moment you start. Winding also lets you check for knots and join a second colour neatly.

How to open a hank without tangles

  1. Untwist the hank and shake it out into one big loop. Find the ties (a few pieces of yarn tied loosely round the strands).
  2. Put the loop round the backs of two chairs, your knees, or a helper's hands, so it's gently taut.
  3. Snip only the ties, never the loop. Find the outside end (it's usually tied to one of the ties).
  4. Wind from there, letting the loop turn on the chairs as you go. If it snags, stop and untangle: pulling makes it worse.

Other ways to wind yarn

  • A simple ball: wrap round your fingers, then round and round in every direction. Quick, but it pulls from the outside and rolls away while you work.
  • On a tube: a nostepinne (a smooth wooden stick) or a cardboard kitchen roll tube does the same job as your thumb. Tuck the tail in a slit at one end, wrap diagonally while turning, then slide the ball off.
  • Ball winder and swift: a ball winder makes a flat-topped "cake" in a minute or two, and a swift holds the hank. Worth it if you buy a lot of hand-dyed yarn; otherwise chairs and your hand are fine.

Winding tips

  • Wind loosely. Yarn wound tight stays stretched; your swatch and project then shrink back when washed. Wrap over two fingers or your thumb to keep slack.
  • Store hand-wound balls loosely and wind wool close to when you'll use it, rather than months ahead.
  • Join leftovers into a scrap ball as you wind: tie or splice them and you've got a ready-made stripe ball. Plan stripes with the random stripe generator.
  • Not sure how much is left? Weigh a part-used ball in the yarn yardage calculator.

How to split a skein in half

Weigh the skein on a kitchen scale and work out half. Wind one ball, putting it on the scale now and then, until it reaches that weight; then cut and wind the rest. The calculator above does the sum, including a split by yards for when a pattern needs a set length. Labels round the weight, and skeins vary by a few grams, so always weigh the real skein rather than trusting the label. Splitting for socks? See the sock size chart for how much each sock needs.

Questions crafters ask

How do you wind a centre-pull ball of yarn by hand?

Hold a 6 in tail under your thumb, wrap the yarn in a figure 8 round two fingers about 20 times, fold the bundle into a core, then wrap diagonally over the core and your thumb, turning a little every few wraps. Tuck the end in and pull the tail from the middle.

Do I need to wind yarn before using it?

Only if it's sold as a hank (a twisted loop). Balls and pull skeins are ready to use; hanks tangle if you knit straight from them.

Should you wind yarn tightly?

No. Winding under tension stretches the yarn, so your project can shrink when washed. Wind loosely over your fingers or thumb.

How do you split a skein of yarn in half?

Weigh it on a kitchen scale, halve the weight, and wind one ball until it reaches that weight on the scale. Cut, then wind the rest.

How do I wind yarn without a ball winder?

By hand with the thumb method, round a cardboard tube or a wooden nostepinne. Put a hank round two chair backs or your knees instead of using a swift.

How long does it take to wind a ball of yarn by hand?

About 10 to 15 minutes for a 100 g skein once you have the rhythm.
