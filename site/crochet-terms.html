US vs UK crochet terms

The same stitch has a different name on each side of the Atlantic. Here's the full conversion, and how to tell which terms your pattern uses.

Quick answer: UK crochet names are one step "taller" than US names. A UK double crochet (dc) is a US single crochet (sc), a UK half treble (htr) is a US half double (hdc), a UK treble (tr) is a US double (dc), and a UK double treble (dtr) is a US treble (tr). Chain and slip stitch are the same in both. If a pattern has "single crochet" in it, it's written in US terms.

How can I tell if a crochet pattern uses US or UK terms?

  • Look for "single crochet" or "sc". UK patterns don't have a single crochet, so if you see one, it's US.
  • Check the spelling and words. "Colour", "tension" and "miss" point to a UK pattern; "color", "gauge" and "skip" point to US.
  • Check the hook sizes. Letter sizes like H-8 suggest US; millimetres only don't tell you much.
  • Read the notes at the top. Most designers say which terms they use.

Why are US and UK crochet terms different?

The two systems count differently. Take the shortest stitch: you put the hook in and pull up a loop, so there are 2 loops on the hook, then you yarn over and pull through once to finish. US names count that finishing pull-through (one, so "single crochet"); UK names count the 2 loops on the hook (so "double crochet"). Same stitch, different counting. Crochet decreases in US and UK terms

Decreases follow the same one-step shift, because they're named after the stitch they're made from. "2tog" means work two stitches together into one.

sc2togdc2togStart a stitch in each of the next 2 stitches, then finish them together. Loses 1 stitch.
hdc2toghtr2togThe same with half double (half treble) stitches.

sc3togdc3togThree stitches into one. Loses 2 stitches.
invisible decreaseinvisible decreaseFor amigurumi: work through the front loops of 2 stitches only. Same name in both.

Is it a mistake if my pattern is in UK terms?

No. Both systems describe exactly the same stitches, and neither is "right". The trouble only starts when you read a UK pattern with US names in your head: every stitch comes out one step too tall and the piece grows much bigger and looser than it should. Pick one system per pattern, and if it helps, write the conversions in pencil at the top. New to patterns altogether? One granny square round, written both ways

Here's the second round of a classic granny square. Same stitches, same square, two sets of names.

US terms: sl st into the ch-2 sp, ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in same sp, ch 1, *(3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 corner sp, ch 1; rep from * around, ending with 3 dc in the first sp, ch 2, sl st to top of ch 3.

UK terms: ss into the ch-2 sp, ch 3 (counts as tr), 2 tr in same sp, ch 1, *(3 tr, ch 2, 3 tr) in next ch-2 corner sp, ch 1; rep from * around, ending with 3 tr in the first sp, ch 2, ss to top of ch 3.

Questions crafters ask

Is UK double crochet the same as US single crochet?

Yes. A UK double crochet (dc) is worked exactly like a US single crochet (sc).

What is a treble crochet in US terms?

A UK treble (tr) is a US double crochet (dc). A US treble (tr) is a UK double treble (dtr).

What does ch-2 sp mean in crochet?

It means the space under a chain of 2 made on the round before. You work the new stitches into that gap, not into the chains themselves. It's written the same way in US and UK patterns.

What is hdc in UK crochet terms?

A US half double crochet (hdc) is a UK half treble crochet (htr). The stitch is worked the same way.

What is sc2tog in UK terms?

A US sc2tog (single crochet 2 together) is a UK dc2tog (double crochet 2 together). It's the same decrease.

Are Japanese and European crochet patterns US or UK?

Many use symbol charts instead of words, which read the same everywhere. Written patterns translated into English can use either set of terms, so check the notes or look for 'single crochet'.

Is a granny square made with double or treble crochet?

Both, depending on the pattern's terms. A classic granny square uses clusters of US double crochet, which a UK pattern calls treble crochet. The stitch is the same.
