Based on the Craft Yarn Council's Standard Yarn Weight System. These are starting points: lace patterns often use bigger needles than the chart suggests, and your own tension changes things too.

How to tell a yarn's weight without a label

Wrap the yarn snugly (not stretched) around a ruler or pencil for one inch, strands touching but not overlapping, and count the wraps. That's the wraps per inch. Roughly: 18 or more is fingering or thinner, 12 to 14 is DK, 9 to 11 is worsted or aran, 7 to 8 is bulky, and 6 or fewer is super bulky.

Swapping one yarn for another

Stay in the same weight number, then knit or crochet a swatch. Two yarns in the same category can still differ, so check the length per 100 g on both labels: if they're close, the swap usually works.

Questions crafters ask

Is DK the same as worsted?

No. DK is weight 3 (light) and worsted is weight 4 (medium), so worsted is a bit thicker. In the UK, aran is close to worsted.

What weight is 4 ply yarn?

In the UK and Australia, 4 ply is about the same as US fingering, weight 1 (super fine).

What does 'weight' mean if it's not grams?

Yarn weight means thickness, not how heavy the ball is. A 100 g ball can be lace or super bulky.
