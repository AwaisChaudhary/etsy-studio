Foundation chain calculator: how many chains to start?

Pick what you're making, your yarn and your stitch. Get the number of chains, the first row written out, how wide it will really be, and about how many rows to reach the length.

Quick answer: chain the number of stitches you need across, plus the turning chain your first stitch skips: +1 for single crochet, about +2 for half double, +2 for double crochet (when the skipped chains count as the first stitch) and +3 for treble. A 36 in baby blanket in worsted double crochet needs about 101 chains (99 stitches). An 8 in scarf in worsted single crochet needs about 25. Swatch first, because your gauge decides the number.

Single crochet starts in the 2nd chain from the hook, half double in the 3rd (chains not counted), double in the 4th (chains count as the first stitch). Your swatch will differ, so use the calculator with your own gauge. Blanket widths are from our blanket size chart.

How do I work out my foundation chain?

  1. Crochet a swatch in the yarn, hook and stitch you'll use, at least 4 in (10 cm) wide, or about 6 in for a blanket.
  2. Count the stitches across 4 in and divide by 4 to get stitches per inch.
  3. Multiply by the width you want. 3 double crochets per inch × 36 in = 108 stitches.
  4. Add the turning chain for your stitch (see below), then round to your pattern's multiple if it has one.

B.Hooked's method skips the maths: chain 10, measure them, and divide the width you want by that length. If 10 chains measure 2.5 in, a 40 in blanket needs 16 sets of 10, so 160 chains. Use the same yarn and hook as the project.

The rule behind the table: chains = stitches + (the chain you start in − 1), minus 1 if the skipped chains count as a stitch. Your pattern's first row tells you which chain to start in, so set that in the calculator and it does the rest. The turning-chain heights (1 for sc up to 4 for treble) follow the usual standard; for taller stitches the turning chain almost always counts as a stitch, for single crochet it doesn't.

What does "chain a multiple of 4 plus 3" mean?

Chain any number you can make from 4 × something, then add 3. So 43, 47 and 51 all work, but 45 doesn't. The plus part already covers the turning chain and the edges, so don't add a turning chain on top. Pick "My pattern says chain a multiple of…" in the calculator and it rounds to the nearest number that fits.

Why is my foundation chain too tight?

Chains are narrower than the stitches worked into them, so a tight chain makes the bottom edge pull in and the piece flare as it grows. Loosen your tension on the chain, or make the chain with a hook one size larger than the pattern asks for and switch back for row 1. A slightly loose chain looks messy at first, but the first row takes up the slack.

Long chain? Chain a few extra. Counting 200 chains is where mistakes happen. Chain 5 to 10 more than you need and put a stitch marker every 20 or 25 chains. Unpick the spare chains from the tail once row 1 is done. Some crocheters skip the chain and use a foundation single crochet row, which makes the chain and the first row together and stays stretchy.

How many rows will my blanket be?

Divide the length you want by the height of one row. Measure it on your swatch: count the rows in 4 in and divide by 4. Taller stitches need fewer rows. In worsted yarn, single crochet is roughly 3 to 4 rows per inch, double crochet about 2 (our estimates, used by the calculator until you change the stitch). Count rows with the row counter.

Questions crafters ask

How many chains do I need for a baby blanket?

For a 36 in baby blanket in worsted yarn, chain about 101 for double crochet or about 109 for single crochet. Bulky yarn needs about 74 for double crochet. Swatch and check your own gauge.

How many chains do I need for a throw blanket?

A 50 in throw in worsted yarn needs about 140 chains for double crochet or 151 for single crochet. Multiply the width by your stitches per inch and add the turning chain.

How many chains for a crochet scarf?

An 8 in adult scarf in worsted single crochet needs about 25 chains, or 24 for double crochet. Children's scarves are about 6 in wide.

Do you add a turning chain to the foundation chain?

Yes. Add 1 for single crochet, 2 for half double and 2 or 3 for double crochet, depending on whether the skipped chains count as the first stitch. If your pattern says chain a multiple plus a number, that number already includes it.

How do I calculate a foundation chain?

Divide the stitches in a 4 in swatch by 4 to get stitches per inch, multiply by the width you want, then add the turning chain for your stitch and round to any multiple your pattern gives.

Why does my crochet get wider at the top?

Usually the foundation chain is too tight, so the bottom edge pulls in. Chain more loosely or use a hook one size larger just for the chain.
