Safety eyes size chart for amigurumi

Which size safety eyes should you buy? Start from the chart by yarn weight, then use the helper: give it the stitches in your head's widest round, and it works out the head size and draws the face at actual size with different eyes, so you can see the look before you snap anything in.

Quick answer: safety eyes are sized by the diameter of the eye in mm. As a starting point (our guide, by yarn weight): 6 to 8 mm for fingering or thread, 8 to 12 mm for DK, 10 to 15 mm for worsted, 14 to 20 mm for chunky and 18 to 30 mm for super bulky or chenille plush. Bigger eyes look younger and cuter; smaller eyes look more realistic. Don't use safety eyes on toys for children under 3 or pets: embroider the eyes instead.

Your hook size and tension change the size of the head, and the head's size matters more than the yarn. If you're between two sizes, the helper above shows both side by side at actual size.

How to choose the right size

  • Head size first. A head with 36 stitches in its widest round is small; one with 60 or more is big, whatever the yarn. The helper turns stitches into a real diameter using a typical gauge for your yarn.
  • Cute or realistic? For a kawaii look, the eye is roughly a fifth of the head's width and set wide and low. For a realistic animal, try about a tenth, set higher and a little closer. These proportions are our own design tips.
  • Common sizes. 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 18 and 24 mm are the easiest to find. Many makers keep 10 to 14 mm in stock because they suit most worsted patterns.
  • Test before you click. Push the eyes in without washers, pin them, and look from a few steps away. Take a photo: faces are easier to judge on a screen.

Where to put safety eyes on amigurumi

Follow your pattern if it gives a round. If not, start a round or two below the middle of the head for a cute face (the widest rounds are a good guide), with about one and a half eye widths between them. Count the stitches between the eyes so both sides are the same, and check they sit on the same round. Put the eyes in before you stuff and close the head: once the washer is on, it's very hard to get off without cutting the post.

Are safety eyes safe for babies?

No: the name is misleading. Safety eyes lock with a washer, but they can still be pulled or chewed out, and a loose eye is a choking hazard. Makers and suppliers advise against them for children under 3 and for pets. Embroider the eyes with black yarn or thread, or use felt sewn on very firmly. If you sell toys, toy safety rules apply where you sell (in the US, the CPSIA; in the UK and EU, the toy safety regulations and CE or UKCA marking), so check them before you list. Our amigurumi pricing guide has more on selling toys.

Safety eye colours and types

Plain black eyes come in two pieces: the eye and the washer. Coloured eyes often have three: the clear eye, a coloured disc behind it and the washer. Put the disc on with the colour facing the eye. There are also oval eyes, cat-slit eyes, glitter eyes and matching safety noses, which go on the same way. Metal washers hold tighter than plastic but are harder to fit; a washer tool or a cotton reel helps you press evenly.

Questions crafters ask

What size safety eyes should I use for amigurumi?

As a starting point: 6 to 8 mm for fingering, 8 to 12 mm for DK, 10 to 15 mm for worsted, 14 to 20 mm for chunky and 18 to 30 mm for super bulky. Smaller for a realistic look, bigger for a cute one.

What size safety eyes for worsted weight yarn?

Usually 10 to 15 mm for a cute look on a 6 to 10 in toy, or 8 to 10 mm for a more realistic animal. 12 mm is a popular all-rounder.

How are safety eyes measured?

By the diameter of the visible eye in millimetres, not including the post.

Can you remove safety eyes once they are in?

Not easily: the washer locks onto the ridges of the post. You usually have to cut the post. Place and check the eyes before you push the washer on.

Are safety eyes safe for babies?

No. They can come out if pulled or chewed and are a choking hazard, so they aren't recommended for children under 3 or pets. Embroider the eyes instead.

Where do you put safety eyes on a crochet head?

Follow the pattern, or start a round or two below the middle of the head, about one and a half eye widths apart, and put them in before stuffing.
