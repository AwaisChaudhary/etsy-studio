How to knit a blanket for beginners

A garter stitch blanket is the friendliest big knitting project there is: one stitch, no shaping and no curling edges. Every step is drawn below, and the writer works out how many stitches to cast on, how many rows to knit, how much yarn to buy and roughly how many hours it'll take, for any size from a lovey to a king.

Quick answer: to knit a blanket, cast on the width you want on a long circular needle (about 135 stitches for a 30 in baby blanket in worsted), knit every row (garter stitch) back and forth until it's as long as you want, then bind off loosely and weave in the ends. Garter stitch lies flat without a border. A 30 x 36 in stroller blanket in worsted garter stitch takes roughly 750 to 1,000 yards of yarn and 18 to 30 hours, by our estimates; chunky yarn is far quicker.

How to knit a blanket, step by step

What you need to knit your first blanket

  • Yarn: a smooth, light-coloured acrylic or wool blend in worsted (4) or chunky (5). Dark and fluffy yarns hide your stitches, which makes mistakes hard to spot. Buy every ball at once and check they share a dye lot number.
  • Needles: a circular needle with a long cable, in the size on the yarn label: 32 in (80 cm) for baby blankets, 40 to 47 in (100 to 120 cm) for throws and bigger. You knit flat on it; the cable just holds all the stitches.
  • Also: a few stitch markers, a tape measure, scissors and a tapestry needle for weaving in ends.

Never knitted before? Start with how to cast on and how to knit and purl, then come back: a blanket is just those stitches, many times over.

Best stitch for a beginner knit blanket

Garter stitch is the easiest: you only ever knit, it's squishy and warm, and the edges stay flat. It does use a bit more yarn and grows slowly in length, because each ridge takes two rows. Seed stitch (knit one, purl one, and knit the purls and purl the knits on the next row) also lies flat and has a lovely pebbly look, but switching between knit and purl is slower. Stockinette (knit a row, purl a row) gives the classic smooth Vs, but it curls hard at every edge, so it always needs a garter or seed border. For more ideas see our 15 knit stitch patterns for beginners.

How many stitches to cast on for a blanket?

Multiply the width you want by your stitches per inch. With worsted yarn at about 18 stitches per 4 in (4.5 per inch), a 30 in baby blanket needs about 135 stitches and a 50 in throw about 225. Chunky yarn at 14 stitches per 4 in needs about 105 and 175. Seed stitch needs an odd number, so the pattern is the same on both sides. Knit a swatch and type your own gauge into the writer for a closer number, or use the cast on calculator for scarves, cowls and pattern repeats.

How to keep the edges neat

  • Slip the first stitch of every row (purlwise, with the yarn in front for garter), then knit the last stitch. This makes a tidy chain-like edge. It's optional, so skip it if it confuses you.
  • Count your stitches every 10 rows. If you have extra, you've probably knit into the strand between stitches or wrapped the yarn over the needle at the start of a row.
  • Pull the first two stitches of each row a little snug to avoid a loose, loopy edge.
  • If you drop a stitch, don't panic: see how to fix a dropped stitch, even several rows down.

How long does it take to knit a blanket?

Much longer than crochet: knit stitches are small, so a blanket is a lot of them. As a rough guide (our estimates for a comfortable knitter), a stroller blanket in worsted garter stitch is about 18 to 30 hours, a throw 50 to 80 and a queen size well over 130. Chunky or super bulky yarn cuts that by half or more, which is why chunky throws are so popular. If you're short on time, crocheting a blanket is about twice as fast. Check a deadline with the knitting time calculator.

Finishing your knitted blanket

Bind off loosely, or the top edge will pull in (our guide to binding off has stretchy options). Weave in every end along the edge or through the garter ridges, then wash it and lay it flat to dry (how to block knitting). Want a picked-up border later? See how to pick up stitches. Check sizes for beds and sofas on the blanket size chart, and if you'll sell it, work out a fair price with the blanket price calculator (it works for knitting too).

Questions crafters ask

How do you knit a blanket for beginners?

Cast on the width you want on a long circular needle, knit every row back and forth (garter stitch) until it's long enough, then bind off loosely and weave in the ends. Garter stitch lies flat, so it needs no border.

How many stitches do I cast on for a blanket?

Width in inches times your stitches per inch. In worsted yarn (about 4.5 stitches per inch) that's about 135 stitches for a 30 in baby blanket and 225 for a 50 in throw.

Can you knit a blanket on straight needles?

Only a small one. A long circular needle (32 to 47 in) is better: you knit back and forth the same way, but the weight rests in your lap instead of on your wrists.

How much yarn do I need to knit a blanket?

Roughly 750 to 1,000 yards of worsted for a 30 x 36 in baby blanket and 2,100 to 2,700 yards for a 50 x 60 in throw in garter stitch, by our estimates. Buy one extra ball.

What is the easiest stitch to knit a blanket?

Garter stitch: you knit every row, both sides look the same and the edges don't curl.

Why does my knitted blanket curl?

Stockinette stitch always curls at the edges. Add a garter or seed border of 5 or more stitches at each side and 8 or more rows top and bottom, or knit the whole blanket in garter or seed stitch.

How long does it take to knit a blanket?

About 18 to 30 hours for a baby blanket in worsted garter stitch and 50 to 80 hours for a throw, for a comfortable knitter (our estimates). Chunky yarn halves it.
