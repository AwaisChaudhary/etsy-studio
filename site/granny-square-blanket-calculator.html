Granny square blanket calculator

How many granny squares you need for any blanket, how to lay them out, and roughly how much yarn the squares, joins and border will take.

Quick answer: divide the blanket width by the size of one square, then the length, and multiply. A 50 × 60 in throw of 6 in squares is about 8 across × 10 down = 80 squares before a border. In worsted yarn, allow roughly a yard of yarn per square inch of blanket, joins and border included, so about 3,000 yards for that throw.

How to work out the number of squares

  1. Make one square and block it. Measure it flat, side to side. Squares from the same pattern can vary by half an inch between crocheters, so measure yours.
  2. Take off the border. If the border will be 2 inches wide, a 50 inch blanket needs 46 inches of squares.
  3. Divide by the square size. 46 ÷ 6 = 7.7, so 8 squares across. Do the same for the length.
  4. Multiply across by down. 8 across × 10 down = 80 squares.

A joining round or a decorative join adds a little to every seam. Over 10 seams a quarter inch each adds 2.5 inches, which is why the calculator asks how you'll join.

Odd numbers look better for patterns with a centre, like a checkerboard or a big diamond. If the count lands on an even number, try a slightly wider border to go down one, or a narrower one to go up.

How much yarn does a granny square take?

A classic 5 inch granny square of five rounds in worsted yarn uses roughly 25 to 30 yards, and finished granny blankets in worsted usually come out at about a yard of yarn for every square inch of blanket, joins and border included. One published 50 × 66 inch throw of 8 inch squares, for example, calls for about 3,300 yards. The calculator uses a little under a yard per square inch for the squares, then adds the joins and border, and scales it all for your yarn weight. Solid granny squares and squares with popcorns take more; lacy squares take less. Treat our numbers as estimates, and if you've made one square already, type its yardage in for a much better answer.

  • Joining takes about 5 to 12 percent of the yarn in the squares, depending on the method.
  • The border is worked in solid rounds, so even a narrow one adds up on a big blanket.
  • Several colours? Each colour only needs to cover its own squares, but buy at least one spare skein of the colour you'll join and border with.

Using up leftover yarn in granny squares

Granny squares are the classic stash buster, since each round can be a different colour. Stick to one yarn weight across the blanket so the squares come out the same size, and keep the joining and border colour the same throughout. To see how far your odds and ends will go, weigh each ball and add them up in the yarn yardage calculator, then divide by the yarn per square above.

Squares versus a one-piece granny blanket

Separate squares are portable and easy to fit in, and you can stop the moment the blanket is big enough. A giant granny square worked as one piece has no seams to sew, but it gets heavy and you can't change its shape later. Either way, check the size against a bed or sofa with the blanket size calculator, keep count of finished squares with the row counter, and see how long it will take in the handmade gift planner. Questions crafters ask

How many granny squares do I need for a throw blanket?

With 6 inch squares, a 50 × 60 inch throw takes about 8 across by 10 down, or 80 squares, before a border. With 4 inch squares it's about 195.

How many 6 inch granny squares for a queen blanket?

About 15 across by 17 down, or 255 squares, for a 90 × 100 inch queen blanket with no border. A 3 inch border on each side brings it down to about 14 × 16, or 224.

How much yarn is in one granny square?

A 5 inch worsted granny square of five rounds uses roughly 25 to 30 yards. For a whole blanket, allow roughly a yard of worsted yarn per square inch of blanket, so about 3,000 yards for a 50 × 60 inch throw. This is an estimate; measure one of yours to be sure.

How many granny squares for a baby blanket?

A 36 inch square baby blanket takes 36 squares of 6 inches (6 × 6), or 81 squares of 4 inches (9 × 9), before a border.

How many granny squares can you make with one skein of yarn?

Roughly 6 squares of 6 inches, or 8 of 5 inches, from a 200 yard skein of worsted, by our estimate. A big 364 yard acrylic skein makes about 11 of 6 inches. Solid squares use more yarn.

Can I mix yarn brands in a granny square blanket?

Yes, as long as they're the same yarn weight and give similar gauge, so the squares come out the same size. Make one square in each yarn and measure before you commit.

Does joining granny squares make the blanket bigger?

Sewn seams and join as you go add almost nothing. A slip stitch or single crochet seam adds roughly a quarter inch per seam, and decorative joins can add half an inch or more.
