Cast on calculator: how many stitches to cast on

Pick what you're making, your yarn and your gauge. Get the cast-on number that fits your stitch pattern, borders and edge stitches, how wide it will really be, and how long to leave the tail.

Quick answer: multiply the width you want by your stitches per inch, then round to fit your stitch pattern. An 8 in adult scarf in worsted yarn (about 18 stitches per 4 in, so 4.5 per inch) needs about 36 stitches. A 50 in throw blanket in worsted needs about 225. Super bulky yarn at 9 stitches per 4 in needs only about 18 stitches for a scarf. Swatch first, because your gauge decides the number.

How do I work out how many stitches to cast on?

  1. Knit a swatch in the yarn, needles and stitch you'll use, at least 4 in (10 cm) wide. Wash and dry it the way you'll treat the finished piece.
  2. Count the stitches across 4 in and divide by 4 for stitches per inch (or by 10 for stitches per cm).
  3. Multiply by the width you want. 4.5 stitches per inch × 8 in = 36 stitches.
  4. Take off the border and edge stitches, round what's left to your pattern's repeat, then add them back.

A few stitches either way rarely matters for a scarf or blanket. It matters more for fitted things like hats and socks, which have their own calculators: hat size and sock size.

What does "multiple of 4 plus 2" mean?

It's how patterns tell you which numbers work. Cast on any number you can make from 4 × something, then add 2. So 22, 26, 30 and 34 all work for "multiple of 4 + 2", but 24 doesn't. A 12-stitch lace repeat worked 10 times is 120 stitches; add a 10-stitch seed border on each side and you cast on 140. Count the border twice, once for each edge.

Knitting in the round usually needs only the multiple, because the round has no edges to balance. If you mix two stitch patterns (say a rib and a cable), the number has to suit both.

Does a garter border change the count? Garter stitch works on any number, so a garter border just adds its stitches to each side. A rib border needs the count to suit the rib too, for example a multiple of 4 for 2x2 rib in the round.

How long a tail for a long-tail cast on?

New to it? Our step-by-step guide to how to cast on knitting shows the long-tail cast on in drawings, with a tail table for every yarn weight. A common rule is about 1 in of tail per stitch for worsted yarn, plus a few inches to weave in. Thinner yarn needs less, thick yarn more, so the calculator scales it by your gauge (our estimate). For a big cast-on, wrap the yarn around the needle 10 times, measure that length, and multiply by the number of stitches ÷ 10. Casting on a blanket? Use two ends of the ball (one from the middle, one from the outside) so you can't run out.

Which needle should I use for a wide cast-on?

Straight needles hold a scarf or dishcloth easily. For a wrap or blanket, use a long circular needle (32 to 40 in, or longer for big blankets) and knit back and forth: the weight sits on the cable instead of your wrists. In the round, pick a circular that is shorter than the piece is around, so the stitches reach without stretching. A 16 in circular suits hats, 24 in suits most cowls, and a long infinity scarf can go on a 32 to 47 in cable.

How many chains for crochet?

Work out the number of stitches the same way, from your crochet swatch. Then chain that number plus the turning chain your pattern's first row asks for (often 1 extra for single crochet, more for taller stitches). Your pattern says which chain to start in, and that decides the exact number. The foundation chain calculator works it out for sc, hdc, dc and treble.

Questions crafters ask

How many stitches do I cast on for a scarf?

For an 8 in adult scarf in worsted yarn at about 4.5 stitches per inch, cast on about 36 stitches. Use about 56 in fingering, 28 in bulky and 18 in super bulky yarn. Children's scarves are 5 to 7 in wide.

How many stitches do I cast on for a blanket?

Multiply the blanket's width by your stitches per inch. A 36 in baby blanket in worsted needs about 162 stitches, and a 50 in throw about 225. Bulky yarn needs about 175 for a throw.

How do I calculate stitches to cast on?

Knit a swatch, count the stitches in 4 in and divide by 4 to get stitches per inch. Multiply by the width you want, then round to your stitch pattern's repeat and add any border and edge stitches.

What does multiple of 4 plus 2 mean in knitting?

Cast on a number that is 4 times something, plus 2: for example 22, 26, 30 or 34. It makes the stitch pattern line up at both edges.

How much tail do I need for a long-tail cast on?

About 1 in per stitch in worsted yarn, plus a few inches, less for thin yarn and more for thick yarn. For a large cast-on, wrap the yarn 10 times around the needle, measure it, and scale up.

How many stitches for an infinity scarf?

Multiply the distance around (usually 50 to 60 in) by your stitches per inch. At 4.5 stitches per inch, 55 in around is about 248 stitches, worked on a circular needle a little shorter than the scarf.
