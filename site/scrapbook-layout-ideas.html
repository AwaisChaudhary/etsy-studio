Scrapbook layout ideas for beginners

Thirty easy scrapbook layout ideas, six drawn steps for putting a page together, and a free layout sketch generator: pick your page size and how many photos you have, and it draws a balanced sketch with exact photo and mat sizes you can follow.

Quick answer: the easiest scrapbook layouts for beginners are a single matted photo with a title, a grid of equal photos with even gaps, and the rule of thirds (photos in two thirds, words and embellishments in the rest). Pick one story per page, choose a plain background and 2 to 3 papers that match the photos, mat the star photo, then add a title, a few lines of journaling and embellishments in groups of three.

30 scrapbook layout ideas

Christmas and winter

  • First snow: one big photo, white cardstock, a few silver snowflakes and the date.
  • Christmas morning grid: 6 small photos in a neat 3 x 2 block with a single red title strip.
  • Advent memories: 24 tiny squares, one per day, with a word under each.
  • The tree year by year: this year's tree photo next to last year's, side by side.
  • Christmas card pocket: a pocket page holding the cards you were sent.

Travel and days out

  • Map background: a printed map of the place, with photos matted in white on top.
  • Ticket strip: train, museum and plane tickets in a row along the bottom.
  • One trip, one page: a big landscape photo across the top and 3 small ones beneath.
  • Postcard spread: a bought postcard as the 'star', with your own photos around it.
  • Day in the life of a trip: times down the side with one photo for each.

Family and baby

  • Month by month: 12 small photos in a grid for a baby's first year.
  • Hands and feet: an outline trace of a hand next to a photo of the day.
  • Then and now: a parent's childhood photo beside their child at the same age.
  • Family recipe: a handwritten recipe card tucked beside photos of cooking it.
  • Favourite things: a list of this year's favourite food, song and toy with one portrait.

School, friends and milestones

  • First day of school: photo, a little interview (age, favourite colour, best friend), and the year.
  • Report card or artwork: shrink and print a child's drawing as the background.
  • Graduation: tassel colours as the paper scheme, cap photo as the star.
  • Best friends: two photos taken years apart, the same pose.
  • Birthday: party photo, the invitation and a candle count in stickers.

Everyday and home

  • Pets: one photo and a list of the pet's silly habits.
  • Our home: a photo of the front door and notes on what you love about it.
  • Ordinary Tuesday: a page with photos from one normal day.
  • Garden through the year: four photos of the same spot in each season.
  • Quote page: a big printed quote with one small photo, lots of white space.

Easy layouts for beginners

  • One photo, one paper: a single matted photo, a title and nothing else.
  • Grid layout: photos in equal blocks with even gaps; very forgiving.
  • Rule of thirds: photos in two thirds, journaling and embellishments in the other third.
  • Colour block: three strips of paper across the page, photos sitting over the joins.
  • White space: lots of plain background, everything grouped in the middle.

Design tips that make any page look better

  • One star: make one photo bigger or matted so the eye knows where to land first.
  • Odd numbers: groups of 3 or 5 (photos, stickers, flowers) look more natural than pairs.
  • Visual triangle: repeat one colour or embellishment in three spots forming a triangle; the eye travels round the page.
  • Even gaps: keep the same space (about 1/4 in) between photos. Grids look neat even with mixed photos.
  • Leave space: white space isn't wasted. A page with room to breathe looks calmer and more modern.
  • Photos first: choose papers to suit the photos, not the other way round, and keep busy patterns away from busy photos.

What size photos for a scrapbook?

Standard 4 x 6 in prints suit 12 x 12 pages (you can fit up to 6 with a little trimming). For 8.5 x 11 and 8 x 8 pages, 3 x 4 in and 4 x 4 in prints fit better, and 6 x 8 albums suit 3 x 4 in or wallet prints. Scrapbook or junk journal?

A scrapbook is built around photos, laid out neatly on flat pages in an album. A junk journal is a hand-bound book stuffed with ephemera, pockets and found paper. Many people do both. Our junk journal vs scrapbook guide compares them, and junk journal page ideas has 42 more layouts if you like the looser style. Journaling stuck? Try our journal prompts.

Questions crafters ask

What is the easiest scrapbook layout for beginners?

A single matted photo with a title, or a grid of equal photos with even gaps. Both look neat with very little planning.

How many photos should be on a scrapbook page?

One to five is easiest. One large photo makes a striking page; 3 to 5 tell a story. A 12 x 12 page fits up to six 4 x 6 in prints with some trimming.

What do you put on a scrapbook page?

A background paper, your photos (matted if you like), a title, a few lines of journaling about who, where and what happened, and a few embellishments such as stickers, die-cuts or washi tape.

What is a scrapbook sketch?

A simple plan of a page showing where the photos, title, journaling and embellishments go. You follow it with your own photos and papers, so you don't start from a blank page.

What size photos are best for scrapbooking?

4 x 6 in prints for 12 x 12 pages; 3 x 4 in or 4 x 4 in prints for 8.5 x 11 and 8 x 8 pages; 3 x 4 in or wallet prints for 6 x 8 albums.

How do I make my scrapbook pages look good?

Pick one focal photo, use papers that share a colour with the photos, keep even gaps, group embellishments in threes in a triangle, and leave some white space.
