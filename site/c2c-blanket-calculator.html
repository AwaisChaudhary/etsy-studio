C2C blanket calculator

Pick a blanket size and your yarn. Get how many tiles wide and tall, when to start decreasing for a rectangle, how many rows and how much yarn to buy. Corner to corner crochet, worked out for you.

Quick answer: divide the width and length you want by the size of one tile. In worsted yarn a tile is about 0.8 in (5 tiles to 4 in), so a 50 × 60 in throw is about 63 × 75 tiles (4,725 in all) and takes roughly 2,300–2,800 yards. A 36 in square baby blanket is about 45 × 45 tiles. Increase until a row has 63 tiles (the short side), work 12 rows that increase at one edge and decrease at the other, then decrease to one tile. Make a swatch: your tile size decides everything.

Count tiles along one straight edge of a small C2C square. Filled in with a typical number until you change it.

How does corner to corner crochet work?

C2C is worked in diagonal rows of small blocks, called tiles or boxes. Each tile is 3 chains plus 3 double crochets. You start in one corner with a single tile, and every row adds one tile until the blanket is as wide as you want. Then you decrease one tile per row until you finish at the opposite corner. Because every tile is a square, a C2C blanket works like a grid of pixels, which is why it's so popular for picture blankets ("graphgans").

  1. Row 1: chain 6, double crochet in the 4th chain from the hook and in the next 2 chains. That's one tile.
  2. Increase rows: chain 6, dc in the 4th chain from the hook and the next 2, then slip stitch into the chain-3 space of the tile below and work ch 3 and 3 dc in that same space for every tile across. Each row ends up one tile longer.
  3. Decrease rows: slip stitch across the top of the last tile instead of chaining 6, then work tiles as before, but don't add one at the end.

How do I make a rectangle in C2C?

Increase at both edges until a row has as many tiles as the short side. After that, keep increasing along the long edge and decrease along the short edge, so each row stays the same length. When the long side reaches its count too, decrease at both edges to the last tile. For a blanket 63 tiles wide and 75 tall:

  • Rows 1 to 63: increase at both edges.
  • Rows 64 to 75: increase on the long side, decrease on the short side (12 rows).
  • Rows 76 to 137: decrease at both edges.

Total rows are always tiles wide + tiles tall − 1. The calculator writes this out for your size, and the drawing shows where each section sits. Keep a stitch marker on the edge that keeps growing; it's easy to forget which side is which.

How big is a C2C tile?

It depends on your yarn, hook and hands. Published patterns give about 5 tiles to 4 in in worsted yarn, 4 tiles to 5 in in bulky and 3 tiles to 5 in in super bulky. To find yours, crochet a small square of about 10 rows, block it lightly, then count the tiles along one straight edge across 4 in (10 cm). Make Do Crew suggests a smaller hook if the tiles have big gaps and a bigger one if they're stiff.

How much yarn does a C2C blanket take?

The table gives our estimates. For a closer number, use Make and Do Crew's method: crochet the first 10 rows (55 tiles), weigh them or measure the yarn used, and divide by 55 to get the yarn per tile. Multiply by the total tiles and add 10 percent. Our yarn yardage calculator turns grams into yards. For a graph blanket, count the squares of each colour on the graph and use the same yarn per tile.

Planning a picture blanket? Your graph needs one square per tile, so a blanket 63 × 75 tiles needs a 63 × 75 grid. Read the graph diagonally from the starting corner. Lots of colour changes? Wind small bobbins for each colour and weave in ends every few rows, not at the end (see how to weave in ends).

Why is my C2C blanket not square?

  • Tension changes between the chain-6 edge and the slip-stitch edge can pull one side in. Keep the chains loose.
  • A miscounted row. Count tiles along each edge at the corner: both edges should have the number you planned.
  • It needs blocking. C2C often looks wavy until it's washed and pinned flat. A border of single crochet or half double crochet around the edge also straightens it.

Questions crafters ask

How many C2C tiles for a throw blanket?

About 63 × 75 tiles (4,725 in all) for a 50 × 60 in throw in worsted yarn at 5 tiles to 4 in, or about 40 × 48 in bulky yarn. Divide each side by your own tile size.

How many C2C tiles for a baby blanket?

About 45 × 45 tiles for a 36 in square baby blanket in worsted yarn, or 38 × 44 for a 30 × 35 in one.

How much yarn do I need for a C2C blanket?

Roughly 1,000–1,200 yards of worsted for a 36 in baby blanket and 2,300–2,800 yards for a 50 × 60 in throw (our estimates). For a closer figure, weigh the first 55 tiles and multiply up.

When do I start decreasing in C2C?

For a square, after the row whose tile count equals the number of tiles across. For a rectangle, decrease on the short side once a row reaches the short side's count, keep increasing on the long side, and decrease on both sides once the long side is done too.

How many rows is a C2C blanket?

Tiles wide plus tiles tall minus one. A blanket 60 × 75 tiles has 134 diagonal rows.

How big is one C2C tile?

About 0.8 in in worsted yarn (5 tiles to 4 in), about 1.25 in in bulky and about 1.65 in in super bulky, from published patterns. Your swatch decides.
