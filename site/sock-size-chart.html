Sock size chart and sock knitting calculator

Foot sizes from baby to men's 14, how much smaller to knit the sock, and the numbers for your cast-on, heel flap, gusset and toe, worked out from your own gauge.

Quick answer: knit socks about 10% smaller around than the foot. A women's medium foot (US 7–9½) is about 8 in around, so the sock is about 7.2 in; at 8 stitches per inch in fingering yarn that's 56 stitches. A men's medium (US 9–11½) foot is about 9 in around, so 64 stitches. Start the toe when the foot measures 2 in less than the whole foot.

How do I measure a foot for socks?

  • Around: wrap a soft tape around the widest part of the foot, across the ball just behind the toes. Keep it snug, not tight.
  • Length: stand on a piece of paper, mark the back of the heel and the tip of the longest toe, and measure between the marks.
  • Making a surprise? Use the chart for their shoe size, or measure a pair of their own socks laid flat (double the width).

The ankle is usually about the same size around as the ball of the foot, so a plain sock keeps the same stitch count from cuff to toe.

How much negative ease should socks have?

About 10%. A sock that's slightly smaller than the foot stretches to hug it and doesn't wrinkle or slip down. Knitty and Mary Maxim both recommend around 10%. Go a little smaller for loose, stretchy fabric and a little bigger for thick yarn, firm stitch patterns or colourwork, which stretch less.

How many stitches do I cast on for socks?

Multiply the foot measurement by 0.9, then by your stitches per inch, and round to a number that fits your rib. A women's medium foot of 8 in × 0.9 = 7.2 in. At 8 stitches per inch that's 57.6, so cast on 56. At 7.5 stitches per inch it's 54, which rounds to 56 too. Thicker sock yarn at 6.5 stitches per inch gives 47, so cast on 48.

Swatch in the round on the needles you'll use, then wash and dry it. Sock gauge is tight (often 28 to 32 stitches over 4 in), so a quarter stitch per inch changes a sock by about 2 stitches.

Heel flap, gusset and toe: the numbers

  1. Leg: knit the cuff and leg to the height you like. The chart gives a standard crew height.
  2. Heel flap: work back and forth on half the stitches. Many patterns make the flap about as many rows as it has stitches, so it ends up roughly square; others use fewer rows for a shallower heel.
  3. Turn the heel, then pick up about one stitch for every two flap rows along each side, plus one in the corner to close the gap.
  4. Gusset: decrease 2 stitches every other round until you're back to the cast-on count.
  5. Foot: knit straight until the sock measures 2 in less than the whole foot (adults). Children's toes are shorter, so the calculator uses about a fifth of the foot length for small sizes (our estimate).
  6. Toe: decrease 4 stitches every other round until about half the stitches are left, then every round until about a quarter remain. Graft the toe closed.

Knitting toe-up? Use the same stitch count for the foot, work the toe increases first, and start the heel when the foot reaches the front of the ankle. Spacing increases or decreases around a cuff? The increase evenly calculator writes the round out for you.

How much yarn do I need for a pair of socks?

One 100 g skein of fingering sock yarn (usually around 400 to 460 yards) makes a pair of plain adult socks in most sizes up to a men's medium. A men's large or a tall leg can need around 530 yards, so buy a little extra. Children's socks often fit in one 50 g ball. Sport and DK socks use fewer yards but more grams. The calculator above gives an estimate for your size and yarn.

What about crochet socks?

Crochet stretches less than knitting, so crochet sock patterns usually leave less negative ease. Measure the same way, then follow your pattern's ease and try the sock on as you go.

Questions crafters ask

How many stitches do I cast on for women's socks?

For an average women's foot (about 8 in around) in fingering yarn at 8 stitches per inch, cast on about 56 stitches. That's 10% smaller than the foot, rounded to a multiple of 4.

How many stitches for men's socks?

An average men's foot (US 9–11½) is about 9 in around, so at 8 stitches per inch cast on about 64 stitches. A large men's foot (US 12–14) needs about 72.

How much negative ease for socks?

About 10%. Knit the sock 10% smaller around than the ball of the foot so it stretches to fit; a little more for loose fabric, a little less for thick yarn or colourwork.

When do I start the toe on a sock?

On a cuff-down adult sock, start the toe decreases when the foot measures about 2 inches less than the full length of the foot. Children's toes are shorter.

How much yarn do I need for socks?

A pair of plain adult socks usually takes one 100 g skein of fingering yarn, roughly 350 to 450 yards. Large men's sizes and tall legs may need more, and children's socks often fit in a 50 g ball.

How long should a heel flap be?

Work the heel flap on half the stitches. Many patterns make it about as many rows as it has stitches, so it's roughly square; some use fewer rows for a shallower heel.
