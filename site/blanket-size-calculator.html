There is no single official blanket standard. These are the sizes most often quoted by crochet and knitting designers, so patterns can differ by a few inches. Bed sizes include some drop over the sides.

How the yarn estimate works

We work out the blanket's area and multiply it by a typical amount of yarn per square inch for plain worsted knitting, then adjust for your yarn: thinner yarn needs more length, thicker yarn less. Crochet usually takes about a quarter more yarn than knitting, cables and bobbles take more again, and open granny or lace stitches take a little less.

The answer is a range, because tension, stitch and borders all change it. Buy enough for the high end, from one dye lot. For hats, scarves and other projects, use our yarn calculator, and if your swatch doesn't match the pattern, the gauge converter shows how big the blanket will really be.

Allow for the border. A few rounds of border can add 5 to 10 percent more yarn on a big blanket. Fringe or tassels add more.

Choosing a size

For a gift, a throw (about 50 × 60 in) is the safest choice: it fits a sofa, the foot of a bed or a lap. For a baby, 30 to 36 inches square is easy to carry and wash. For a bed, measure the top of the mattress and add the drop you want on each side, usually 10 to 15 inches.

Questions crafters ask

What size is a crochet throw blanket?

Most throws are about 50 × 60 inches (127 × 152 cm). Some patterns go up to 54 × 72 inches for a longer sofa blanket.

What size is a baby blanket?

A baby or receiving blanket is usually 30 to 40 inches square. A stroller blanket is a little smaller, about 30 × 36 inches, and a crib blanket can be as big as 45 × 60 inches.

How much yarn for a queen size crochet blanket?

Very roughly 6,700 to 8,500 yards of worsted weight yarn for plain crochet, which is often 34 to 43 skeins of 200 yards. Chunky yarn needs far fewer yards. Use the calculator for your own yarn, and treat it as an estimate.

How many skeins for a throw blanket?

In worsted yarn, a knitted throw takes roughly 1,800 to 2,200 yards and a crocheted one about 2,300 to 2,800 yards, so roughly 10 to 15 skeins of 200 yards once you allow a little spare. These are estimates.
