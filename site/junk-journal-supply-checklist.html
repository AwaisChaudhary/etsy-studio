Junk journal supply checklist

Everything you need to start a junk journal, and the 16 things you probably already have at home. Tick what you've got, pick a budget and get a short shopping list, plus a free pocket and tag template to print.

Quick answer: to start a junk journal you need paper, a cover, something to stick with, scissors and thread (or a stapler) to bind it. That's it: Stampin' Up's starter list is paper, scissors or a trimmer, adhesive, pens and ephemera. Most of it is free from home: old envelopes, book pages, cereal boxes, receipts and tickets. If you buy anything first, make it a good glue stick, washi tape and a big-eye needle. Ink pads, stamps, stickers and printables are extras you can add later.

The journal itself
  • Printer paper, old book pages, envelopes, brown paper bags, wrapping paper, maps or sheet music.
  • An old hardback book cover, a cereal box covered in paper, or a folded piece of card.
  • To sew the folded pages in. Embroidery floss, waxed thread, twine or strong string.
  • A tapestry or bookbinding needle to sew the pages. Pricker for holes optional. Free swap: a stapler for small booklets.
  • An awl, a push pin or a thick needle for the sewing holes.
Cutting and folding
  • Everyday scissors do almost everything.

Sticking things down
  • The everyday adhesive for paper on paper. Least likely to wrinkle thin pages. Free swap: school glue spread thin.
  • Heavier things: fabric, lace, buttons, card.
  • Clean, flat and instant. Tape runners are the easy version. Free swap: glue stick.
  • Sticks and decorates at once, and lifts off again. Free swap: strips of paper and glue.

Ephemera and pretty things
  • The everyday paper that tells the story of a day.

  • Cut from card with the top corners snipped.

Colour, ageing and writing
  • Any pen for journaling; a fine black pen for labels.

What supplies do I need to start a junk journal?

Only five things are must-haves: paper, a cover, an adhesive, scissors and a way to bind it (thread and a needle, or a stapler for a thin booklet). Stampin' Up's list of basics is paper, a trimmer or scissors, adhesive, pens or markers, and ephemera, with stamps, ink, ribbon and embellishments as add-ons. 101 Planners groups supplies the same way: paper, ephemera, decorations, writing tools and adhesives.

What can I use for free?

  • Pages: printer paper, old book and magazine pages, envelopes, wrapping paper, brown bags, maps and sheet music.
  • Cover: the cover of a worn-out hardback, or a cereal box covered with paper or fabric.
  • Ephemera: receipts, tickets, tags, labels, postcards, packaging, napkins and stamps from your post (101 Planners). Our 62 ephemera ideas has a list to tick off.
  • Decoration: ribbon and lace from gift wrap, buttons, fabric scraps, and pressed leaves and flowers.
  • Ageing: a cup of tea. Our tea staining guide shows how.

What's worth buying first?

Our picks for a small first shop: a glue stick (dries flat on thin paper), a tacky glue for lace and buttons, a roll or two of washi tape, and a big-eye needle for sewing signatures. A bone folder and an ink pad are the next most useful. 101 Planners suggests paper of about 100 gsm or more if you'll write or paint on the pages, so ink doesn't show through.

What glue is best for a junk journal?

  • Glue stick: paper on paper. Thin, flat and quick.
  • Tacky or liquid glue: fabric, lace, buttons and heavy card. Use a thin layer so pages don't buckle.
  • Double-sided tape or a tape runner: instant and flat, good for photos and pockets.
  • Glue dots: small heavy things like buttons and charms.

Our tip: put heavy pages under a book while they dry so they stay flat.

How do I bind the pages?

Fold a few sheets in half, nest them together into a "signature", poke 3 or 5 holes along the fold and sew through them into the cover. Our signature calculator works out how many sheets, where the holes go and how much thread you need. For a first try, a thin booklet stapled down the middle works too.

Questions crafters ask

What supplies do I need to start a junk journal?

Paper, a cover, an adhesive such as a glue stick, scissors, and thread and a needle (or a stapler) to bind it. Ephemera like tickets and receipts, washi tape and pens are the next things to gather.

Can I make a junk journal for free?

Yes. Use printer paper, envelopes and old book pages for the pages, a cereal box or old book cover, string to sew it, and receipts, tickets and packaging as ephemera.

What is the best glue for junk journals?

A glue stick for paper on paper, tacky glue for fabric, lace and buttons, and double-sided tape or glue dots for instant, flat hold.

What paper is best for junk journal pages?

Any paper works, and mixing types is part of the look. For pages you'll write or paint on, paper of about 100 gsm or heavier stops ink showing through.

Do I need a sewing needle to make a junk journal?

A big-eye tapestry or bookbinding needle is easiest for sewing signatures. For a thin first journal you can staple the fold instead.
