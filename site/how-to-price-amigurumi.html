How to price amigurumi

Crochet toys are small but slow: tight stitches, lots of little parts and a face to sew on. This calculator estimates the hours from the toy's size and parts, adds stuffing, eyes and fees, and gives you a price that pays for your time.

Quick answer: price amigurumi like any handmade item: materials + (hours × your hourly rate), plus about 10% for overheads, plus selling fees. The catch is the hours: count the time to make and sew on every part, stuff it and do the face, not just the body. By our estimate a 6 in (15 cm) toy in worsted yarn with 6 parts takes about 4 hours, so with $5.50 of materials at $15 an hour it comes to about $75 (about $85 on Etsy). A simple keyring takes about 1 hour and is about $25. Many toys sell for less than this, so smaller and simpler toys are how sellers hit lower prices without working for free.

What's the formula for pricing crochet toys?

  1. Materials. The share of each skein you used, plus stuffing, safety eyes, a tag and packaging. Hostinger's pricing guide suggests adding 10 to 15% to the yarn for waste.
  2. Time. Every minute: crocheting, stuffing, sewing on parts, the face, and packing. Hours × an hourly rate, at least your local minimum wage.
  3. Overheads. A percentage for hooks, photos, your shop and trips to the post office.
  4. Fees. The shop's or card reader's cut, added on top so it doesn't come out of your pay.

The full method, with craft fair and consignment fees, is in our guide to pricing crochet items.

Why doesn't "materials × 3" work for amigurumi?

Because a toy uses very little yarn. June Gilbank of PlanetJune points out that a toy made from under half a ball of yarn can take as long as a blanket made from 5 or 6 balls, so tripling the materials badly underprices it. The last column of the table shows what "materials × 3" would pay you an hour. She also argues a full hourly wage can make toys hard to sell, and aims for a middle ground that covers materials, time and how complex the design is.

How long does it take to crochet an amigurumi?

From under an hour for a tiny toy to a few days for a big, detailed plush. One market seller on the Ribblr forum makes small chickens and bunnies in under an hour each. Our handmade gift planner uses about 6 hours for a small toy. The parts take longer than people expect: a body is one piece, but four legs, two arms, two ears and a tail are nine more pieces to crochet, stuff and sew on. Time yourself on the next one, start to finish, and use that.

What do crochet toys actually sell for?

Often less than the formula says. The Ribblr seller above priced chickens at $5 to $8 and bunnies and octopuses at $8 to $12 for a small market, where shoppers had little to spend. Prices like that only work for toys that take under an hour. Ways to close the gap:

  • Make a small, quick range: keyrings, minis and one-piece toys that take under an hour, for buyers who want something under $15.
  • Keep bigger toys for buyers who value them: gifts, custom pets and characters, and online shoppers looking for something special.
  • Use thicker yarn for big plush: chunky chenille covers a big toy in about half the stitches (see the table).
  • Don't undercut other makers. PlanetJune warns against it; selling faster than you can make, or sore hands, are signs your price is too low.

How do I price a custom amigurumi order?

  • Charge for the design time. Working out a new character, sketching and test pieces is real work (Hostinger).
  • Set fixed add-ons for common requests, such as a name embroidered on, a scarf or a different colour, so you don't quote from scratch each time (Hostinger).
  • Take payment before you start and give a finish date; see the custom order tips in our pricing guide.

Can I sell toys made from someone else's pattern?

It depends on the designer. PlanetJune lets people sell toys made from her patterns as long as they credit her as the designer; other designers don't allow it at all. Check the pattern's terms before you list, and credit the designer in your listing when they ask for it. Your own designs are yours to sell, and you can charge more for them.

Are there safety rules for selling crochet toys?

Yes. Toys made for children are covered by toy safety laws in most countries, handmade ones included. In the US, start with the Consumer Product Safety Commission's Online Sellers Safety Guide; in the UK and EU, look up the toy safety rules for your country. Our tips: use safety eyes with locking backs and give each a firm tug test, sew parts on securely, and embroider eyes and noses for toys meant for babies and young children. If a toy is a decoration, not for children, say so clearly in the listing. This is general information, not legal advice.

Questions crafters ask

How much should I charge for an amigurumi?

Add materials, your hours times an hourly rate, about 10% for overheads, then selling fees. By our estimate a 6 in (15 cm) toy with 6 parts takes about 4 hours, which at $15 an hour is about $75. Keyrings and one-piece toys can be under $25.

How long does it take to crochet an amigurumi?

From under an hour for a mini or keyring to several hours for a 6 in toy with limbs and ears, and a day or more for a large plush. Sewing on parts often takes as long as the body.

Is materials times 3 a good way to price crochet toys?

No. Toys use little yarn but a lot of time, so materials times 3 can pay you a few dollars an hour. Price your hours instead.

Can I sell amigurumi made from a pattern?

Only if the designer allows it. Some, like PlanetJune, allow sales with credit to the designer; others don't. Check the pattern's terms.

Do I need safety eyes or embroidered eyes for toys I sell?

For toys meant for babies and young children, many makers embroider the eyes. If you use safety eyes, use ones with locking backs and check the toy safety rules where you sell.

How do I price custom crochet toys?

Add your design time, use fixed prices for common add-ons like a name or a scarf, take payment before you start and give a finish date.
