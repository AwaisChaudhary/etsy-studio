How to make junk journal tags (free printable template)

Tags are the easiest junk journal embellishment: a scrap of card, a hole and a ribbon. This guide shows six simple steps, 30 tag ideas, the standard tag sizes, and a free template maker that prints a full sheet of tags in your size and shape.

Quick answer: to make junk journal tags, cut a rectangle of card (a #5 shipping tag is 4 3/4 x 2 3/8 in), clip the two top corners at 45 degrees, and punch a hole about 1/2 in from the top. Age it with tea or ink if you like, layer on scraps, a picture or washi tape, and tie ribbon or twine through the hole. Make tags about 1/4 to 1/2 in narrower and 1/2 to 1 in taller than the pocket they'll sit in, so the top and ribbon stick out.

30 junk journal tag ideas

  • Paper: book pages, sheet music, old maps, envelopes, receipts, tea bag wrappers, ledger paper, seed packets, paper doilies, gift wrap.
  • Layers: a strip of washi tape across the middle, a scrap of lace, a torn edge of patterned paper, a stamped image, a vintage picture from our ephemera ideas, a pressed flower under tape.
  • Finishes: tea or coffee stain (see how to tea stain paper), inked edges, a sewn zigzag border, a wax seal, a metal eyelet, crinkled seam binding.
  • Uses: a pocket insert, a bookmark tied to the spine, a hidden journaling spot, a dated memory tag, a list of the month's favourite things, a quote, a gift tag that becomes a page.
  • Seasonal: Christmas tags with gold ink and a sprig of greenery, autumn leaves, Valentine hearts in red tissue, spring botanicals.

Pockets and tags together

Most tags live in pockets. Make the tag about 1/4 to 1/2 in narrower than the pocket so it slides in easily, and 1/2 to 1 in taller than the pocket, so the top with the hole and ribbon sticks out where you can grab it. Enter your pocket size above and the maker tells you if the tag fits. For pocket shapes and sizes, see junk journal pockets, and for more page layouts junk journal page ideas.

What card to use

Use card stock of about 65 to 110 lb (176 to 300 gsm), so tags hold their shape in a pocket. Free options work just as well: cereal and cracker boxes (glue paper over the printed side), file folders, old greeting cards and manila envelopes. Thin paper tags are fine for layers, but back them with card. New to all this? Start with our junk journaling for beginners guide and supply checklist.

Questions crafters ask

How do you make tags for a junk journal?

Cut a rectangle of card, clip the two top corners, and punch a hole about 1/2 in from the top. Age or decorate it with paper scraps, pictures and washi tape, then tie ribbon or twine through the hole.

What size are junk journal tags?

Most are shipping tag size #3 to #5 (3 3/4 x 1 7/8 in to 4 3/4 x 2 3/8 in). A #8 tag (6 1/4 x 3 1/8 in) makes a large journaling tag. Make tags about 1/4 to 1/2 in narrower and 1/2 to 1 in taller than the pocket they go in.

What paper is best for junk journal tags?

Card stock of about 65 to 110 lb (176 to 300 gsm), or free card like cereal boxes, file folders and old greeting cards. Thin paper works for layers on top.

What do you put on junk journal tags?

Scraps of book pages, sheet music or patterned paper, a vintage picture, washi tape, lace, stamps and a few handwritten words. The back is a good hidden spot for journaling.

Can I print junk journal tags?

Yes. The template maker above prints a full sheet of tags on US Letter or A4 paper in your size and shape. Print at 100% and cut along the lines.

How do you age junk journal tags?

Brush them with cold tea or coffee, dab distress ink round the edges, or crumple the card and flatten it again. Let them dry flat under a heavy book.
