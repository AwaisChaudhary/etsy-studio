Free crochet snowflake pattern (easy, 4 rounds)

This little six-point snowflake is only four rounds, so it's a good first snowflake: a ring of double crochet, a round of chain loops, a round of shells, then picot points. Make it in cotton thread for a delicate tree decoration or in yarn for a quick window snowflake, then stiffen it so it stays flat.

Quick answer: to crochet an easy snowflake, work 12 dc into a magic ring, then 6 chain-3 loops, then a shell (2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in each loop, and finish with a picot point (sc, ch 3, sc, ch 6, sc, ch 3, sc) in each shell. To make it stiff, soak it in white PVA glue mixed half and half with water (or liquid starch), squeeze it out, pin every point flat on a board covered in cling film and leave it to dry overnight. In size 10 cotton thread with a 1.75 mm hook it's about 3 in (7.5 cm) across; in worsted yarn about 5.5 in (our estimates). Each snowflake takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

How to crochet a snowflake, step by step

What you need

  • Thread or yarn: white or ice-blue cotton holds its shape best and takes stiffener well. Size 10 crochet thread gives the classic lacy look; size 3 cotton or a DK cotton is easier for beginners because you can see the stitches. Acrylic works, but it never goes quite as crisp.
  • Hook: 1.5 to 1.75 mm for size 10 thread, 2.25 to 2.5 mm for size 3, or the label size for yarn.
  • To finish: a stiffener (see below), a bowl, an old towel, a foam board, cork board or folded cardboard covered with cling film or a plastic bag, and rust-proof pins.

Abbreviations

ch chain, sl st slip stitch, sc single crochet (UK double crochet), dc double crochet (UK treble), sk skip, st stitch, *…* repeat the part between the stars. A ch-3 loop is the space under a chain 3. New to these? See basic crochet stitches, the magic ring and joining with a slip stitch.

How to stiffen crochet snowflakes

All the usual methods work the same way: soak, squeeze, pin, dry. The difference is how stiff the snowflake ends up and how it copes with damp.

StiffenerHow to mix itResult
White PVA glueAbout equal parts glue and water (more glue for stiffer)Firm, lasts for years, dries clear. The easiest choice.
Liquid laundry starchUse straight from the bottleMedium stiff; washes out, so you can re-shape it next year.
Cornflour pasteStir about 1 tablespoon cornflour into a little cold water, add to 1 cup of boiling water, stir until it thickens a little, coolMedium; cheap and kitchen-safe. Can soften in damp rooms.
Sugar syrupEqual parts sugar and water, warmed until the sugar dissolves, cooledVery stiff, but goes sticky in humid air and can attract insects. Store in a dry box.
Spray starchPin the snowflake first, then spray until damp; repeat once dryLight hold; good for big yarn snowflakes or a quick touch-up.
Ready-made fabric stiffenerAs the bottle saysVery firm and reliable; the most expensive.
  1. Soak the snowflake in the mixture for a minute, working it in with your fingers.
  2. Squeeze out the extra (don't wring it), then blot it in a towel so the holes aren't filled with glue.
  3. Pin it face up on your covered board. Put a pin in the centre of the ring first, then one in each of the 6 points, then the side picots. Use a printed hexagon or a ruler so the points are evenly spaced.
  4. Dry it completely, usually overnight (up to 24 hours for glue in a cool room). Don't use a hair dryer on sugar syrup.
  5. Unpin and check: if it's still floppy, give it a second, slightly stronger coat.

Our guide to blocking knitting and crochet covers pinning and spray blocking for larger pieces.

Snowflake problems and fixes

  • It cups or curls: your chains are tighter than your stitches. Go up a hook size for round 2, or chain more loosely; stiffening also flattens it.
  • It ruffles: the shells are too big for the loops. Use 1 dc either side of the ch 2 instead of 2.
  • Points are uneven: count the chains in every point (3, 6, 3) and pin them all to the same circle when you stiffen.
  • White glue left a film in the holes: poke it out with a pin while it's still wet, and dilute the glue a little more next time.

Questions crafters ask

How do you crochet a simple snowflake for beginners?

Work 12 double crochet in a magic ring, then 6 chain-3 loops, then a shell of (2 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in each loop, and finish with a point of (sc, ch 3, sc, ch 6, sc, ch 3, sc) in each shell. Stiffen it and pin it flat to dry.

How do you stiffen crochet snowflakes?

Soak them in white PVA glue mixed about half and half with water (or liquid starch), squeeze out the extra, pin every point flat on a board covered with cling film and leave them to dry overnight.

Can you stiffen crochet snowflakes with cornstarch?

Yes. Cook a thin cornflour paste (about a tablespoon in a cup of water, heated until it thickens slightly), cool it, soak the snowflakes, pin and dry. It gives a medium hold and can soften in damp rooms.

What thread is best for crochet snowflakes?

Mercerised cotton crochet thread in size 10 for a classic lacy snowflake, or size 3 cotton for an easier, slightly bigger one. Cotton holds stiffener better than acrylic.

How big is a crochet snowflake?

This 4-round pattern is about 3 in across in size 10 thread, 3.75 in in size 3 cotton and 5.5 in in worsted yarn (our estimates).

How long does a crochet snowflake take?

About 20 to 30 minutes for this pattern once you know it, plus overnight drying after stiffening.
